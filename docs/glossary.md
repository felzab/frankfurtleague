## Core entities

### `Saison` — the competition year

**Is:** the year everything else hangs off, carrying the `rules` that configure how it is played — and, outside `rules`, two windows: `bewerbung`, when a school may apply to play it, and `registrierung`, when a pupil may register for it.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/saisons/schemas.py :: FLSaison` over the writable half both payloads share, `:: _SaisonWritable`; the two windows are `:: FLSaisonBewerbung` and `:: FLSaisonRegistrierung`, and the schedule the rules imply is `fl_backend/app/api/saisons/schedule.py :: knockout_phases_for`.\
**Trap:** the id is a short fixed-length string rather than an ObjectId (`fl_backend/app/shared/schemas/bounds.py :: SAISON_ID_LENGTH`), and every model that ACCEPTS one AS DATA TO STORE holds it to that length. Two kinds deliberately do not: a model echoing a stored id, because one refusing a stored row would answer 500 for the whole list it appears in, and a LIST FILTER, which matches rather than stores. So an id of the wrong length that reaches the database by a route holding it to nothing is echoed back without complaint, while every match and matchday carrying it fails to read. The one payload that MINTS an id is tighter than that length — four ASCII digits naming a year the league can play — so a create refuses an id the read models go on serving. `_SaisonWritable` declares `bewerbung` and `registrierung` with NO default, which `FLSaison` inherits as declared, so a PATCH omitting either key is refused rather than silently closing a window somebody opened; and neither span is judged against the season's own: a window may legitimately open before the season does and close long before its first fixture.

### `Spiel` — one match

**Is:** the central entity — fixtures, results, the league table and the bracket are all views of matches.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpiel` stored, `:: FLSpielJoined` served to the base tier and `:: FLSpielJoinedAdmin` to the match editor; the frontend reads the two served shapes as the generated `fl_frontend/src/core/generated/client/types.ts :: FLSpielJoined` and `:: FLSpielJoinedAdmin`.\
**Trap:** both teams are embedded rather than referenced, so a club rename fans out into the matches of every season that is not `past` and a finished season keeps the name it was played under; the embedded name rides on no payload and is composed from the season's junction row on every write; either side is null while its occupant is unknown; and `/spiele` has no POST and no DELETE — a season's fixtures are written by its draw and removed only by a confirmed replace of that draw or an undraw of it, both season-scoped and declaring neither verb.

### `Spieltag` — matchday, fixture round

**Is:** a block of matches inside a season, carrying a phase and, once somebody sets one, a date range.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spieltage/schemas.py :: FLSpieltag`, ordered by `fl_backend/app/api/spieltage/services.py :: order_spieltage`, labelled by `fl_frontend/src/features/spieltage/utils.ts :: spieltagLabel`.\
**Trap:** `position` is STORED and orders matchdays within one PHASE, so the numbers restart at 1 in every round and `uniq_saison_id_saison_phase_position` is what keeps two matchdays off one slot — the season's draw writes it and no payload carries it afterwards, and nothing renumbers a matchday — a confirmed replace removes the season's whole list and draws a fresh one, and an undraw removes that list and writes none back; `beginn` and `ende` are null until an admin dates the matchday, and an undated one states no span for a fixture to fall outside of; the name and the match count carry no field at all, the label being composed by the reader and `anzahl_spiele` derived from the season's rules, which is why neither can be sorted on.\
**See:** the `Spielplan` entry below for what creates a matchday and what removes one.

### `Team` — club

**Is:** a club. The `teams` document is **season-independent** and carries the club as it stands today; its group, its `austritt`, the name and shorthand one season was played under, and its table are assembled at read time from a junction row and from the matches.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLTeam`, flattened by `fl_backend/app/api/teams/services.py :: build_team_pipeline`.\
**Trap:** the junction join is strict and there is no other kind — `build_team_pipeline` refuses a call carrying no `saison_id` rather than joining leniently, a season-less join returning one row per season played and a table of zeros beside it — so a team with no `saison_teams` row for the season disappears from the results entirely rather than appearing with an empty table; and the `name` that read serves is the junction's, so a club renamed after a season finished still reads there under the name it played under. `GET /teams/saisons` is the club-centric read and serves the club's own name instead. The people who run the league are never a `Team` on screen: their page is „Organisation“, at `/organisation`.\
**See:** [`domain.md`](domain.md) for the aggregate.

### `Spieler` — player

**Is:** a person, whose season-specific facts — squad membership, captaincy, retirement — live on a `saison_spieler` junction rather than on the person.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spieler/schemas.py :: FLSpieler` is the stored person flattened against one season and reaches no endpoint; `:: FLSpielerPublic` is what `fl_backend/app/api/spieler/services.py :: build_spieler_pipeline` projects for the base tier.\
**Trap:** of a person's name only `vorname` is required, the surname admitting null, and `nummer` is a string rather than a number — a write takes one to four digits (`fl_backend/app/api/spieler/schemas.py :: SQUAD_NUMMER_PATTERN`) while the read model takes whatever is stored, so a hand-edited row still lists; the base tier is served a name only where the person's consent record publishes it (`READ-PUPIL-003`) and a surname only as an initial (`READ-PUPIL-001`), is served no `stufe` (`READ-PUPIL-002`), no birthdate, no consent record, no sign-in address (`READ-PUPIL-004`) and neither `ist_nachnominiert` nor `rolle`, so no public squad list names a captain; `email` is stored as `fl_backend/app/shared/folding.py :: sign_in_identifier` folds it and never as it was typed, which is what makes the seam joining a signed-in person to their row an equality, and only the admission and an address change taking effect write it — one address naming one person under `uniq_spieler_email` — so a new address waits in `adresswechsel`, signing in to nothing, until the change takes effect, which may be well after its mailbox confirmed it (`fl_backend/app/api/adresswechsel/crud.py :: move_the_address_in_session`, [`ops/runbooks.md`](ops/runbooks.md#5-when-somebody-asks-for-their-data-or-asks-us-to-change-it)), and registering again from another address writes a second person; and `FLSpieler` is one player against one season and carries no `saison_id`, so the admin list reads `GET /spieler/saisons` instead.

### `Kader` — one team's squad in one season, as its own contact seats see it

**Is:** every `saison_spieler` row one team holds for one season, live and ausgetragen alike, which any of that team's contact seats reads on their panel and edits row by row; the season's `max_kadergroesse` caps the live ones.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spieler/schemas.py :: FLKaderResponse` over `:: FLKaderZeile`, read and edited through `fl_backend/app/api/spieler/person_router.py`; the page is `fl_frontend/src/features/spieler/components/views/KaderView.tsx` and its writes `fl_frontend/src/features/spieler/personActions.ts`.\
**Trap:** the word names four things sharing no shape: these rows; an application's `kader`, the school's own ESTIMATE in two counts, naming nobody and checked against no squad afterwards (`fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbungKader`); a pupil's own `kader` on their account read, every squad row of theirs across seasons under the name each club played under (`fl_backend/app/api/spieler/schemas.py :: FLSpielerSelfKaderZeile`); and the consent scope `kader_oeffentlich`, which publishes a name on the PUBLIC squad list, a different read again. A seat holder is served each surname whole where the public list carries an initial, and nobody's address or telephone number (`READ-KADER-001`); an ausgetragen row is read-only to them, only an administrator bringing it back; and a squad number two live rows wear is marked `nummer_doppelt` and never refused ([`domain.md`](domain.md#what-the-model-permits-on-purpose)). The English half of the code says `squad`, so a search for one spelling finds none of the other's sites.\
**See:** the `Spieler` entry for the person a row hangs off, and the `aufnehmen` · `Aufnahme` entry for the write that adds a registered pupil.

### `Schiedsrichter` — referee

**Is:** a referee, embedded on a match as `{schiedsrichter_id, name, payment}` — the shape the DOCUMENT stores, which the match payload does not mirror and which a base-tier fixture read serves without the `payment` and with the name reduced past its first space (`READ-REFEREE-001`), so a name carrying no space arrives whole and an erased referee's fixture carries no name at all.\
**In code:** the `schiedsrichter` collection — `fl_backend/app/core/collections.py :: Collection`; the embedded copy is `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpielSchiedsrichterField`, narrowed to `:: FLSpielSchiedsrichterFieldPublic` for the base tier.\
**Trap:** deletion is soft (`inactive_since`) but a privacy request is not — every match embeds the referee's name and id, so an erasure repoints every match that named them at the ghost, one permanent retired row (`fl_backend/app/core/sentinels.py :: GHOST_SCHIEDSRICHTER_ID`) whose null name every surface reads as „anonym“, and deletes the document, where a pupil, whom no match embeds, is erased outright; a match still to be played keeps its booking on the ghost and surfaces as a retired booking, `booking_unknown_resource` refusing the ghost every new fixture, so the erasure never meets `schiedsrichter_still_assigned`; the ghost answers not found on every by-id admin route, and no erasure reaches it, each selecting the records an address holds; a rename must fan out into the embedded copy on every match carrying it — every season's, unlike a club's, because a referee is not season-scoped; and the collection's own read is admin-tier, a referee being a pupil whose `kontakt` and `schule` are private (`READ-CONTACT-001`) beside a `default_payment` that is money (`READ-MONEY-001`); and a row carries its own confirmation link and consent record, so every referee an administrator enters is mailed that link by the create itself, the address being required on every write for exactly that reason, and a retired referee is asked nothing until reactivated; and the public fixture read joins that record, serving the name, reduced, only where it publishes it, and the referee only once they agreed where they were asked about the fixture, so „anonym“ reads for a withheld name as for an erased referee.

### `Spielort` — venue, playing location

**Is:** a venue, embedded on a match as `{spielort_id, name, maps_link, mietpreis}` — the shape the DOCUMENT stores, which the match payload does not mirror and which a base-tier fixture read serves without the `mietpreis`.\
**In code:** the `spielorte` collection — `fl_backend/app/core/collections.py :: Collection`; the embedded copy is `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpielOrtField`, narrowed to `:: FLSpielOrtFieldPublic` for the base tier.\
**Trap:** `maps_link` is **not** a URL despite the name — free text built server-side and searched on Google Maps, so it carries no scheme check — and it is the venue's whole postal address in one string, composed by `fl_backend/app/api/spielorte/admin_router.py :: _maps_link` from the name and the address's own `fl_backend/app/shared/schemas/addresses.py :: to_string`. It rides on every base-tier fixture read that names a venue, while the collection's own read, structured `address` included, is admin-tier (`READ-ADDRESS-001`).

### `Bewerbung` — a school's application to play a season

**Is:** one school's application to play one season, stored as it arrived apart from what the confirmation flow moves: the triage moves `status`, `entscheidung` and `team_id`; each seat's own person fills their `geburtsdatum` and consent record, or answers with a `Widerspruch` and empties the slot, and may later withdraw their WhatsApp scope and media consent from their account page; an administrator's two repairs move one seat's address or seat another person where one stepped out; a re-send replaces the entry under `bestaetigungen` of every seat that person holds and restarts `bestaetigungsfrist`; and a contact erasure empties the slot naming the person who asked for it, with the entry beside it. The opponent it would like on the first Spieltag is a FREE STRING and never a reference: a school may name an applicant nobody has accepted yet, and a picker over the accepted ones would give a later applicant the longer list.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbung`, the stored shape; `POST /bewerbungen` in `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/public_router.py` is what creates one, and the two decisions are `POST /bewerbungen/{bewerbung_id}/annehmen` and `.../ablehnen` in `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/admin_router.py`. A stored one is written afterwards by its seats' own people through `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/public_router.py` and `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/person_router.py :: patch_einwilligung`, by an administrator through the re-send and the two repairs beside the decisions, by an erasure of a seat's person through `fl_backend/app/api/kontakte/crud.py :: loeschen_kontaktperson_in_session`, and by the clocks through `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/system_router.py`; and the create itself writes to a stored one, where its `Idempotency-Key` is one a stored application already carries.\
**Trap:** **while the application still stands `eingereicht`**, exactly one of `team_id` and `schule` carries a value — the club the applicant picked, or the school they propose — and nothing in the store can say so, which is why acceptance judges it and refuses the rest (`bewerbung_subject_unresolved`) rather than branching on an assumption. Acceptance is IRREVERSIBLE, `saison_teams` having no DELETE, so a second decision on either endpoint is refused (`bewerbung_already_decided`) rather than entering the club again; it writes the created club's id back into `team_id` and leaves `schule` where it stood, so a decided application always names a club and an accepted new school's names both. One base-tier route serves stored fields of an application: the confirmation view answers the seat a link opens — its state, the school, the season and every role its holder answers for through that link, with the label its page runs, which follows from who seated them, and not their name, their birthdate, their consent or anything a leaked link could act on (`READ-BEWERBUNG-002`). Everything else is admin-tier, unlike `spiele`: an application holds three people's email addresses and telephone numbers, a date of birth per seat that stays null until that person enters it, and says which schools asked and were turned down. The public form's create is base-tier too, and answers the deadline it wrote and one raw link per seat, never the hashes it stored (`READ-BEWERBUNG-001`) — or, replaying a stored key, no link at all where one may already be in an inbox.\
**See:** [`domain.md`](domain.md) for the boundary this collection is held true in.

### `Registrierung` — one pupil's registration for a team's season

**Is:** one pupil's registration to play one season for one team, submitted through the link an administrator minted for that team and decided later. It holds a forename, a surname, the address the league mailed the confirmation link to, and — once that link is answered — a birthdate and a consent record. `status` has **two** stored members, `eingereicht` and `abgelehnt`: an admission deletes the row, in the same transaction that writes the person and the squad row from it.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/registrierungen/schemas.py :: FLRegistrierung`, the stored shape; `POST /registrierungen` in `fl_backend/app/api/registrierungen/public_router.py` is what creates one, `POST /registrierungen/bestaetigung` beside it is what the pupil answers, and `POST /registrierungen/sweep/{saison_id}` in `:: system_router.py` is what erases one by the clock, a person's erasure taking every one at their address (`fl_backend/app/api/registrierungen/crud.py :: loeschen_registrierungen_in_session`). The admin read is `GET /registrierungen` in `:: admin_router.py`; the team's own read, its admission and its decline are in `:: person_router.py`, and the pupil's own withdrawal before the admission is on that file's `spieler_router`.\
**Trap:** it is not a `Bewerbung` — that is a SCHOOL applying to play, decided by the league, while this is a PUPIL registering to play for a school already entered — and the two share no collection and no deadline. Nothing about a pending registration reaches `spieler` or `saison_spieler`, so a registered pupil is on no squad list and counts towards no squad cap until a seat holder of the team admits them, and the cap is judged at the submission against the squad rather than against the registrations beside it: two pupils may register for one remaining place and both land, the admission judging the cap again and admitting one. A registration whose address a stored person already holds IS that person, admitted into them rather than beside them, and the team is asked first only where the name or the birthdate differ; a name alone only proposes. The submission key outlives an admitted row on the squad row it became, so a replay after the admission stores nothing. The stored `email` is the sign-in fold every address payload stores, as `spieler.email` is, so the two join by equality and a row typed in by hand in another spelling matches neither. No late-entry marker is stored here at all: whether a pupil joined a season already under way is a fact about the squad entry the admission writes, and the invite's own read answers the PERIOD instead. A registration whose address and name are a stored person's who confirmed their record opens the returning pupil's page, which asks no choice: its record is the stamp and the label alone, the choices standing on the person, so a reader taking `umfang` off a confirmed registration finds none. A mistyped address is not corrected: the row goes with the seven-day sweep and the pupil registers again through the same link.\
**See:** the `Bewerbung` entry above for the application this is not, the `Einwilligung` · `Kenntnisnahme` entry below for the record a confirmation writes, and [`domain.md`](domain.md) for the boundary this collection is held true in.

### `aufnehmen` · `Aufnahme` — a team taking one registered pupil into its squad

**Is:** a contact seat's decision on one confirmed `Registrierung` of their team: in one transaction it writes the person the registration's address resolves to, or a new one, and that person's row in the season's `Kader`, and deletes the registration. It is the one write that creates a player.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/registrierungen/person_router.py :: aufnehmen`, composing through `fl_backend/app/api/registrierungen/services.py :: compose_person`, `:: compose_person_update` and `:: compose_kader_fields`; the frontend's action is `fl_frontend/src/features/registrierungen/personActions.ts :: aufnehmenRegistrierungAction`.\
**Trap:** it is not `annehmen`, the administrator's acceptance of a school's `Bewerbung`: this one is a team's own seat holder's, authorised by their seat through a person's binder and by no grant, and the two declines share the verb `ablehnen` and nothing else. Into a stored person it writes the registration's name, birthdate and address and renews their consent choice by choice, each only where the registration set it later, so a withdrawal the pupil made after registering stands; a returning pupil's registration asked no choice and no birthdate, so it renews nothing and leaves the person's birthdate standing, and where its address holds no stored person it admits nobody (`registrierung_person_fehlt`): the pupil registers again. It clears a retired person's stamp and rewrites their retired squad row of the season whichever team held it, while a live row anywhere that season refuses it, and it judges the squad cap and the season's Stufen again, the submission's verdict on either being no promise. The registration's log images are redacted in the same transaction, so nothing of the pending row outlives the admission but the submission key the squad row takes over.\
**See:** the `Registrierung` entry above for what it admits, and the `Kader` entry for where the row lands.

### `Aktion` — one recorded write, and what it replaced or removed

**Is:** a row of the `aktionen` collection — one write, carrying the actor it is attributed to — an admin session, a signed-in person under a pseudonym and the kind of person the write was made as (a contact person, a pupil, a pending registrant among them, or a referee, and never a Funktion a panel grants: an account-only person holds none and still withdraws a consent), the public where the request authenticates nobody, or the system where no request made it at all — the route, the collection, the operation, the image of what the write replaced or removed, and, on an administrator's erasure, the `anlass` naming the request it answers.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/recording.py :: record_write` appends it from `fl_backend/app/core/crud.py`, which every write passes through, and `fl_backend/app/core/recording.py :: act_row` builds the one row whose `collection` names an act rather than a collection, `anmeldungen`: an administrator ending an address's sessions, which writes only the sign-in store and keeps no address; `fl_backend/app/api/aktionen/schemas.py :: FLAktion` is the shape served back.\
**Trap:** `actor.email` is an address for an administrator alone — a sentinel for the system and the public, and absent from a signed-in person's actor, which no address reaches — and once that person or administrator is erased, `actor.email` or `actor.pseudonym` on every row they wrote under the erased address holds `fl_backend/app/core/recording.py :: GELOESCHT_ACTOR_MARKER`, which names nobody, while a row they wrote under an earlier address of theirs keeps that address's pseudonym until the log's twelve months take it. Each fan-out is ONE row, carrying the filter it ran and a count and no pre-image at all, so nothing can restore a document from it — and a club rename issues a fan-out per collection pass beside the row for the club itself, every one of them sharing that rename's trace id, which is what gathers them into a single action on the page; and that filter is text rendered for a reader rather than a query anything can replay. A removal is the one row carrying an image PER document it took, or none at all where it was an erasure, whose image would be a fresh copy of exactly what the erasure destroyed. And a row outliving its subject is the point everywhere but a person: every write that destroys a person's values reaches in here in the same transaction, overwrites that person's values inside each image holding them and stamps `redacted_at`, the people beside them keeping theirs where the document survives, while a write removing the document empties its images whole, and the row outlives that erasure until the database expires it, twelve months after the write
where the row carries the stamp the expiry reads.\
**See:** [`domain.md`](domain.md) for why the collection sits in no consistency boundary.

### `Sperrliste` — the addresses barred from signing up and from the league's mail

**Is:** one row per barred email address, holding an HMAC-SHA256 of the canonicalised address under a key derived for this list, the label that key was derived under, the administrator's reason, who entered it, the day, and the last season the ban covers. The BARRED address is stored nowhere and no row yields it without the key. A ban lapses on its own five full seasons after the one it was entered under, and outlives the erasure of the person it bars and of the administrator who entered it until it does.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/collections.py :: Collection`'s `SPERRLISTE` member, stored as `fl_backend/app/api/sperrliste/schemas.py :: FLSperrlisteEintragRecord` and keyed by `fl_backend/app/core/constraints.py :: UNIQUE_INDEXES`' `uniq_sperrliste_adresse_hash`. `fl_backend/app/shared/folding.py :: canonical_address` canonicalises an address, `fl_backend/app/api/sperrliste/services.py :: adresse_hash` computes the stored form, `:: find_sperrliste_refusal` answers `duplicate_key` and `:: find_verwaltung_refusal` refuses an administrator's address as `sperrliste_verwaltung`; `fl_backend/app/api/sperrliste/crud.py :: address_is_gesperrt` is the lookup, each handler reaching it and the two below through the request's `fl_backend/app/api/sperrliste/lookup.py :: get_ban_list`, asked by the ban's own write, by a grant, by a registration, by the two repairs of an application's contact seats, by the referee writes that mint a link, and by the subject read and the sign-in gate's `POST /identitaet/anmeldung`, each answering it as `gesperrt` beside the records; `:: gesperrte_hashes` is its batch, asked by an application for its seats, and `:: gesperrte_adressen` hashes stored addresses for it, asked by an application's re-send, the three confirmation presses and their views, both sweeps' reminders, the grants' revoke, tier change and claim, every admin read naming an author, which withholds what it finds, the actor check on every admin-tier request, the person check every person's binder carries (`fl_backend/app/api/sperrliste/guard.py :: verify_person_is_unbarred`), the season's invite press and its preview (`fl_backend/app/api/saisons/admin_router.py :: _gesperrte_empfaenger`), the administrator's reveal, which answers it to the ban's dialog (`fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/admin_router.py :: get_loeschung_ansicht`), and the Postausgang's hand-out, which judges every mail's address at the last moment before its send, every kind but the ban's own notice (`fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/system_router.py :: post_ausgabe`); the ban's create also ends the address's live sign-ins through `fl_frontend/src/core/auth.ts :: endSessionsOfAddress`; `fl_backend/app/api/saisons/crud.py :: pull_massgebliche_saison_id` answers the season each of them compares against.\
**Trap:** „sperren“ is what an administrator does to an address and never to a person, and the row is a record about two people all the same: `erstellt_von` holds the administrator's own address in plain, the one field on a served row carrying an `@`, and `grund` is free text that may NAME the person barred — an address in it is refused (`fl_backend/app/api/sperrliste/schemas.py :: refuse_an_address_in_the_reason`), and a name never is. Both are served — `erstellt_von` withheld where the ban list holds that administrator too — and copied into a removal's log image; `grund` is kept past either person's erasure, `erstellt_von` until its administrator's, which leaves `GELOESCHT_ACTOR_MARKER` in its place, and neither can be produced for the person barred, nothing in the row naming the address it was taken from. The KEY is the whole security of the list, and it can never be rotated: every stored hash was taken under it and no address survives to re-hash, so a replacement disarms every ban in silence, the check computing a hash no row holds while the list still renders. **The secret file `sperrliste_schluessel` holds not that key but the master it is derived from**, one label per corpus, so a digest taken under the master directly belongs to no corpus and matches nothing. A row's bound and the season it is read against move in ONE write: the activation that runs past a ban removes it in the same transaction, so no ban on the served list is one that bars nobody — and a lapsed row reached any other way would pass `address_is_gesperrt` while `uniq_sperrliste_adresse_hash` still refused a fresh ban of the address. What is hashed is not what a payload stores: the address is stored with its domain in punycode and keyed with it decoded, the form the stored bans were keyed on, a Greek-sigma domain apart, so a key taken off the stored spelling matches no ban.\
**See:** [ops runbooks §5](ops/runbooks.md#5-when-somebody-asks-for-their-data-or-asks-us-to-change-it) for the procedure that creates a row and what losing the key costs.

### `Einladung` — the link one team registers its players through

**Is:** one minted registration link for one team's season, stored as a row of `einladungen` — the season, the team, an unkeyed SHA-256 of the copied link and of every mailed one, each live, the administrator who minted it and the day, and a revocation date that is null while it stands. That administrator is the one person it names, their address held in plain until their erasure leaves `GELOESCHT_ACTOR_MARKER` in its place, and nothing deletes the row.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/collections.py :: Collection.EINLADUNGEN`, stored as `fl_backend/app/api/einladungen/schemas.py :: FLEinladungRecord`; minted and revoked by `fl_backend/app/api/teams/admin_router.py :: post_einladung` and `:: delete_einladung`, a season at a time by `fl_backend/app/api/saisons/admin_router.py :: post_einladungen_versand`, and judged by `fl_backend/app/api/einladungen/services.py`. The link a visitor opens is spelled by `fl_frontend/src/core/einladungEmail.ts :: einladungsLink`.\
**Trap:** it is not a `Bestätigung` — nobody confirms an Einladung, and it carries no person to confirm: what it authorises is submitting one pending `Registrierung`, which one of the team's three contact seats then admits. **The raw link value survives only in the mint's own answer**, so a link nobody copied out of that response is lost and the repair is another mint rather than a read. It expires with the season's registration window, and for good once the season has ended, and carries no date of its own, so moving that window moves every link with it; and a REVOKED row is kept rather than deleted, which is why every lookup asks for a null `widerrufen_am` and a hash alone finds a link that is spent. Whether one was ever MAILED is read off the newest Postausgang row owed about it (`fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/crud.py :: lagen_of`) and never off the row's existence.\
**See:** the `Registrierung` entry for what a link opens, and `Zustellstand` for what became of its last mail.

### `Berechtigung` — one address's access to the Verwaltung, stored

**Is:** a row of `berechtigungen`: one folded address, the tier it holds as `verwaltung`, who granted it and when. The one Funktion that is stored rather than derived, there being no league record to derive it from. Beside it `berechtigungen_angekuendigt` holds what every administrator has been told the rows are, or will be, keyed on each row's own id, and the Postausgang holds each change and each request still to be mailed, one row per recipient. A grant made while another administrator could approve it, and an owner's promotion while another owner could, waits in `berechtigungen_antraege` (`Antrag`) until that person does.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/collections.py :: Collection`'s `BERECHTIGUNGEN`, `BERECHTIGUNGEN_ANGEKUENDIGT` and `BERECHTIGUNGEN_ANTRAEGE` members, keyed by `fl_backend/app/core/constraints.py :: UNIQUE_INDEXES`' `uniq_berechtigung_adresse`; granted, revoked and moved between tiers by `fl_backend/app/api/berechtigungen/admin_router.py`, a request's approval among them, moved to a changed address by `fl_backend/app/api/adresswechsel/crud.py :: _move_the_grant`, compared, stamped and owed to the Postausgang by `fl_backend/app/api/berechtigungen/system_router.py :: post_berechtigungen_abgleich`, removed with every notice and record naming its holder by `fl_backend/app/api/berechtigungen/loeschung.py :: loeschen_grants_and_authorship_in_session`, and read on every admin-tier request by `fl_backend/app/api/berechtigungen/guard.py :: verify_actor_is_admin`; listed, granted, revoked and moved between tiers on `fl_frontend/src/app/bereich/admin/administratoren/page.tsx`, and reconciled by `fl_frontend/src/features/berechtigungen/abgleich.ts :: runBerechtigungenAbgleich`, whose notices the Postausgang's drain sends.\
**Trap:** the address must be stored FOLDED, and nothing but the application's own write folds it: the store refuses no spelling, so a row the Playground typed in capitals is stored and admits nobody, every reader reading it as no grant at all, and nothing counts or names it, so the paste that writes a grant folds its address first. A row written or deleted in the database leaves no log row, which is why the notice reads a record of what was announced rather than the log — so deleting an announced row by hand re-announces its grant as new. Its `erteilt_am` is not when it took effect: a Playground row's is whatever the paste typed, never read as earlier than its `_id`'s time, so the reconciliation stamps a grant it finds with `gefunden_am`, and that dates it wherever it stands — a session signed in before either admits nobody. Nor do its dates or its id survive an edit: a row the reconciliation has read carries `gesehen_am`, and one edited since — its address changed in place, or put back after its removal — admits nobody until that pass finds it again. A row an address change moved is granted anew, so a session dates from the move, while its `fortgefuehrt` keeps the replaced row's dates for every four-eyes judgement: the standing is the person's, not the mailbox's. It is not a `Sperrliste` row: a barred address is granted nothing and a granted one is banned by nothing. On screen a grant is „Zugang“, and no other visitor-facing text uses the word: an account is „Konto“ and signing in „Anmeldung“.\
**See:** the `verwaltung` entry for the tiers, and [ops runbooks §3](ops/runbooks.md#3-granting-or-revoking-admin-access) for granting an owner.

### `Antrag` · `berechtigungen_antraege` — a grant or a promotion waiting on a second administrator's approval

**Is:** a row of `berechtigungen_antraege`: one folded address, the tier it is to hold, the grant a promotion names, and under `genehmigung` who asked (`gestartet_von`), when (`angefragt_am`) and until when (`bis`). A grant, or an owner's promotion of an administrator, writes one instead wherever another administrator could approve it, another owner for a promotion; that approval writes the grant or the tier and removes the request in one transaction. Whoever may approve it may refuse it, and its requester may withdraw it; one nobody decides lapses at `bis`. On screen „Antrag“.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/collections.py :: Collection`'s `BERECHTIGUNGEN_ANTRAEGE` member, keyed by `fl_backend/app/core/constraints.py :: UNIQUE_INDEXES`' `uniq_berechtigung_antrag_adresse` and removed after `bis` by `:: TTL_INDEXES`' `berechtigungen_antraege_bis`; asked by `fl_backend/app/api/berechtigungen/admin_router.py :: post_berechtigung` and `:: patch_berechtigung`, decided by `:: post_antrag_genehmigung` and `:: delete_antrag`, judged by `fl_backend/app/api/berechtigungen/services.py :: genehmigung_verwehrt` and `fl_backend/app/api/berechtigungen/antraege.py :: antrag_steht`, and removed with its addresses by `:: loeschen_antraege_in_session`; listed under „Offene Anträge“ by `fl_frontend/src/features/berechtigungen/components/collections/AdminBerechtigungAntraegeList.tsx :: AdminBerechtigungAntraegeList` and decided by `fl_frontend/src/features/berechtigungen/actions.ts :: genehmigeAntragAction` and `:: lehneAntragAbAction`.\
**Trap:** a row's presence says nothing: the TTL monitor removes a lapsed one up to a pass late and a restore puts one back, so every read filters on `bis`. A request stands only while its requester holds the tier it asks for since before it, so a revoke, a demotion or a ban leaves it approved by nobody, offered for refusal, and the requester's own address change does not, while a change of the address it is for ends it as a refusal does. Where nobody else could approve, the act takes effect at once, announced: the administrator the database's first owner grants, and a lone owner's promotion of a second owner, ask nobody. An address change moving a grant is no new grant and writes no request, its approval being the change's own (`Adresswechsel`). The collection is named as the grant's other member is, `berechtigungen_angekuendigt`, because it belongs to the `Berechtigung` aggregate, its approval writing the grant beside it in one transaction; the compound „Berechtigungsantrag“ would read as a root of its own, as `spielbestaetigungen` is. It is no „Anfrage“: „Löschung auf Anfrage“ and „Adressänderung auf Anfrage“ answer a person's request to the league, while an Antrag is one administrator's act waiting on another.\
**See:** the `Berechtigung` entry for the grant it makes.

### `Löschung` · `loeschung` — deleting what the league holds about a person, on a request or by the season-after rule

**Is:** one of two routes through the same erasure, differing in what they reach: „Löschung auf Anfrage“, which a person asks for or an administrator runs answering one, takes every record held at the address; the season-after rule's deletion, run when a season is activated and finished by an hourly pass, takes a pupil's own data alone — the record, every squad row and every registration at the address, and their log authorship as a pupil — once no squad row of theirs sits in a kept season and no registration of theirs waits for a decision.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/crud.py :: loeschen_everything`, run on a request by `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/admin_router.py :: post_loeschung` (`POST /identitaet/loeschung`) and by the person's own `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/person_router.py :: post_konto_loeschung` (`POST /identitaet/konto/loeschung`), the rule's scope being `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/crud.py :: NUR_SPIELER`; the rule's run is `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/system_router.py :: sweep_loeschung`, judged by `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/services.py :: loeschung_faellig`, and its count before the press `fl_backend/app/api/saisons/admin_router.py :: get_loeschung_vorschau`. Identifiers spell it `loeschung`, the journal's token `fl_backend/app/core/journal.py :: JOURNAL_TOKEN` among them; the values stored or logged keep their English: the action log's `erase_many` operation and `ERASED` actor marker, and the pass's `erasure_unanswered` field.\
**Trap:** the two routes are not interchangeable after a restore. A requested erasure journals one line so it can be run again; the rule's deletion journals none, the rule deleting the same pupils again on its own, and running one of them as a request would take the whole address — a contact seat, a referee row or a grant held there included. The rule selects on seasons and never on `inactive_since`, so a retired row is still never purged on its age; and either route deletes the documents outright and empties their log images, so nothing restores a deleted pupil but a snapshot.\
**See:** [`datenschutz.md`](datenschutz.md#6-retention-is-bounded-where-a-bound-was-chosen) §6 for the retention it delivers, and [ops runbooks §13](ops/runbooks.md#13-after-a-restore-from-a-snapshot) for a restore.

### `Spielbestätigung` · `Spielbestaetigung` — one party's answer about one Ansetzung of a fixture

**Is:** one party's answer about one `Ansetzung` of one fixture — a team's, given by whichever of its three contact seats presses its link first, or a referee's, or a Zusage an administrator who heard it elsewhere records for the party — stored as a row of `spielbestaetigungen` beside the snapshot of the Ansetzung it answers about, the deadline it was given (`frist`), the links each seat was mailed, the answer (`antwort`: agreed, or declined, a club proposing another date and a referee giving a reason, and who answered by ROLE, never by name — a seat, the referee, or `admin` with the administrator's required reason — beside `halter`, a keyed digest of a seat holder's address that no read serves and that the person's erasure or Widerspruch matches to null the reason they typed) and, once it stops applying, why (`geschlossen`). At most one row per party per Ansetzung stands open.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/collections.py :: Collection.SPIELBESTAETIGUNGEN`, read through `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/schemas.py :: FLSpielbestaetigung` and stored as `:: FLSpielbestaetigungRecord`; the token lookup and the two readings of "open" are `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/services.py`, and the frontend's mirrors `fl_frontend/src/features/spielbestaetigungen/schemas.ts`.\
**Trap:** it is not a `Bestätigung`, though its link lives in that family's address range, `/bestaetigung/spiel`: a Bestätigung is one person's answer to their own link about their own record, while this is a club's answer about a fixture's date, given by whichever seat presses first, and it confirms nothing about the person pressing — which is why the address reads as the compound and never as „Bestätigung“ alone. "Open" means two things: the answer and the unique index read a row as open while neither `antwort` nor `geschlossen` holds a value, and the sweep's close while `geschlossen` alone is empty, so an answer a move overtook still records why it stopped applying. A club's decline the administrator discarded is closed `verworfen` and still counts: as the club's one answer to that Ansetzung, which the sweep never asks again, and as its agreement on the public card, the league having kept the date, until the sweep closes it again as it closes an agreement beside it, for a decision, a call-off, a club's exit or its day passing, never for a move. Its club is mailed that the date stands, and its links read `verworfen` while the fixture keeps that date. `frist` is the day the team's answer is due, `SPIEL_BESTAETIGUNG_FRIST_TAGE` before the match or the day it is asked where the match is nearer than that (`fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/services.py :: frist_of`), stored per row so a changed bound moves no request already sent; it is not the link's lifetime counted from a mint. A referee's row stores one too and is bound by it nowhere: a referee answers until the fixture's day, which is all their Einteilung states. A referee's own decline closes their row `schiedsrichter_abgelehnt` in the write taking them off the fixture, so it reads as their decline rather than as a change of referee, and booking them again asks them afresh. A row of a fixture called off reads `abgesagt` on its link and on the referee's panel ahead of any answer, as one about an Ansetzung left reads `ungueltig`: an agreement shown would send its party to the ground. A no-show entered before the fixture's day counts as a call-off here, its parties told so, though the `sonderereignis` decides the fixture; entered on the day or after, it closes the row `spiel_entschieden` and tells nobody.\
**See:** `Ansetzung` for what a row answers about, `vorschlag`, `halter` and `Zusage` for three of its words, `Bestätigung` for the person's own link it is not, and [`domain.md`](domain.md#what-is-held-true-together) for why it stands outside the season's boundary.

### `Spielbericht` — one played fixture's report, by its referee or an administrator

**Is:** one document per fixture in `spielberichte`, its `bericht` the account of the match the booked referee, or an administrator, writes: a proposed result (`ergebnis_vorschlag`), one scorer entry per goal, the cards with their colour and, on a caution, their reason, and remarks for what no field holds. The whole block is one proposal, and the fixture's result is never written from it: the administrator carries it into the editor and saves it there.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/collections.py :: Collection.SPIELBERICHTE`, read through, and stored as, `fl_backend/app/api/spielberichte/schemas.py :: FLSpielbericht`.\
**Trap:** it is not the referee's own: `eingereicht_von` names whoever wrote it first, `admin` where an administrator did, and every later write keeps it, while the referee who reads it, and after the season reads it alone, is the one booked now. A null scorer is one nobody named and nothing else: an erasure never nulls an entry, which would read as one. A report on a fixture called off or struck out after it was sent reads `abgesagt`, awaiting nobody. Its goals and entries are by side, and `seiten` holds the clubs each side was when it was last written: a knockout correction fielding another club since makes it read `teams_geaendert`, which no save adopts until the report is written again about the clubs playing. `version` counts the block's writes and the adoption stores the one it took (`uebernommene_version`); it is no `Fassung`, which is a consent wording. Its `ergebnis_vorschlag` is a result, never the date a club's `vorschlag` proposes, and adopting it (`uebernimmt_bericht`, `uebernommen_am`) is never carrying a club's proposed date into the Ansetzung, though the editor names both presses „Vorschlag übernehmen“. The public result stays `spiele.ergebnis` alone.\
**See:** the `Ergebnis` entry for the result the administrator saves, and [`domain.md`](domain.md#what-is-held-true-together) for why the report stands outside the season's boundary.

### `Postausgang` — every mail the league owes, one row per recipient

**Is:** the one queue every mail leaves through: a row names the mail's kind, its recipient, the facts its builder renders and the record and seat it is about, and is written in the transaction of the write that owes it; the drain hands each row out under a lease of its own, minting any link the mail carries, and the row then records what became of the message.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/collections.py :: Collection.POSTAUSGANG`, stored as `fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/schemas.py :: FLPostausgangZeile`, written by `fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/services.py :: enqueue_mails` and drained through `fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/system_router.py`.

## Attributes and values

### `Tore` — goals

**Is:** the goals each side scored, `None` while the match is unplayed.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpiel`.\
**Trap:** in the statistics the pair is `tore_geschossen` and `tore_kassiert` — scored and conceded, not two spellings of one count.

### `Ergebnis` — result, final score

**Is:** the score as a string — `"3:1"`, or null when unplayed — derived server-side from the two `tore` values.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpiel`, read by the frontend as the generated `fl_frontend/src/core/generated/client/types.ts :: FLSpielJoined`.\
**Trap:** both ends constrain it to `^[0-9]+:[0-9]+$`, the class deliberately `[0-9]` rather than `\d`, whose Unicode-aware backend reading would accept digits `Number()` turns into `NaN`; the frontend matches that pattern rather than splitting on `":"`, because a bare colon would read as a 0:0 draw.

### `Elfmeterschießen` — penalty shoot-out

**Is:** `elfmeterschiessen` on a match — the shoot-out's own scoreline, null on every match that did not finish level and on every no-show, whose award is composed rather than played.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpielElfmeterschiessen`, read by the frontend as the generated `fl_frontend/src/core/generated/client/types.ts :: FLSpielElfmeterschiessen`.\
**Trap:** its readers disagree on purpose — the bracket takes a winner from the counts so a level knockout advances a side, and a club's own page names that winner in its rounds and fixtures (`fl_frontend/src/features/teams/utils.ts :: computeEntscheidungFor`), while the league table and every figure a page counts never consult them and score the fixture as the draw it was; a level shoot-out is refused, and a record stored against a group match or a decided one is discarded.

### `Gruppe` — a group within a season

**Is:** the closed set `A` through `P` on the `saison_teams` junction; a season runs the first `rules.number_of_groups` of them.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLGruppenNames`, read by the frontend as the generated `fl_frontend/src/core/generated/client/types.ts :: FLGruppenNames`.\
**Trap:** the grouped response is seeded from `rules.number_of_groups` rather than from the teams present, so a group holding nobody keeps its key and a group the season does not run has none; and teams arrive already in standing order, so re-sorting a group anywhere is a second answer to who finished second.

### `Spielplan` — a season's whole draw

**Is:** every matchday and every fixture of one season, composed in one operation from that season's `rules` and the clubs entered into it: round _k_ of every group is matchday _k_, so the groups play in step, and each knockout round that follows is one further matchday whose sides are `Quelle` references rather than teams. Nothing it writes carries a date.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/saisons/spielplan.py :: draw_spielplan` composes the documents, `fl_backend/app/api/saisons/services.py :: find_spielplan_refusal` decides whether a season may be drawn at all, and `fl_backend/app/api/saisons/schemas.py :: FLSaisonSpielplan` is the watermark the season keeps afterwards.\
**Trap:** the watermark is a record rather than the guard, so a repeat draw is measured against the FIXTURES and one made by any other route is caught too — which is what lets a season drawn by hand be replaced at all. A second draw is refused unless the request CONFIRMS a replace, which `spielplan_replace_outside_its_window` holds to a `future` season with nothing recorded: it removes the season's fixtures and matchdays and draws fresh ones in one transaction. An UNDRAW removes the same two lists in that same window (`spielplan_undraw_outside_its_window`) and clears this field rather than restamping it, which is what returns a season to undrawn. The season's creation writes no key, so the field is defaulted on the record model, a missing key reading as undrawn just as a stored null does.\
**See:** `fl_backend/app/api/saisons/services.py :: find_spielplan_refusal` and `:: find_undraw_refusal` for the refusals they raise, and [`domain.md`](domain.md) for what an undraw reopens.

### `spiel_nr` — a match's number within its season

**Is:** an `int` with `gt=0` on a match, and a `sort_by` key.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/constraints.py :: uniq_saison_id_spiel_nr`.\
**Trap:** it is unique within a season rather than globally, and every season starts again at 1 — which is why `resolve_bracket` must be given exactly one season, and why a `Quelle` references a match by this number rather than by `_id`.

### `saison_phase` — stage of the season

**Is:** the stored set `gruppenphase` · `achtelfinale` · `viertelfinale` · `halbfinale` · `finale`, declared in bracket order so each knockout round feeds the next.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: PHASE_ORDER`; which rounds a season plays is `fl_backend/app/api/saisons/schedule.py :: knockout_phases_for`.\
**Trap:** `"playoffs"` is **not** one of them — a query-only alias compiled to `saison_phase != "gruppenphase"`, on no stored document; and `GET /teams` takes `statistik_scope` rather than this filter.

### `spiel_status` — match status

**Is:** the filter's `ab_heute` · `vergangen` · `heute` · `abgesagt` · `unbekannt`, and the label's `ausstehend` in place of `ab_heute`, derived on both ends and stored nowhere.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpielStatus`, compiled by `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/services.py :: build_spiele_filter`; `fl_frontend/src/features/spiele/types.ts :: SpielStatus`, derived by `fl_frontend/src/features/spiele/utils.ts :: computeSpielStatus`.\
**Trap:** the two differ on purpose — the server's `ab_heute` is `datum >= today` and **includes** today, the client's `ausstehend` is `datum > today` and **excludes** it, because a filter selects while a label partitions; `unbekannt` as a filter returns everything, no branch matching it; and `abgesagt` is not the whole of `sonderereignis` — an abandoned fixture took place, so it reads by its date like any other. And „Vorläufig“ is no value of it: the chip lays that word over `ausstehend` from the served confirmation verdict, which no filter reads.

### `sonderereignis` — what happened to a fixture beyond being played

**Is:** `ausgefallen` · `nichtantreten_team1` · `nichtantreten_team2` · `abgebrochen` · `annulliert`, or `null` where there is nothing to say.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSonderereignis`, with one named member set per consumer beside it.\
**Trap:** it is not a delete — the match keeps its row, its `spiel_nr` and its bracket slot — and no member of it reaches the figures the table is scored and sorted on; a no-show counts because its goals are composed from `rules.forfeit_ergebnis`, while `ausgefallen` and `annulliert` may carry no result at all. The one figure the field decides is `anzahl_abgesagte_spiele`, which counts the fixtures that did not take place — every member but `abgebrochen`.

### `Paarung` — the two sides of one fixture and what they produced

**Is:** which two clubs meet in one fixture, and what they produced: each side's club and goals, the shoot-out record and the `sonderereignis`, the four fields a bracket resolution can rewrite on a fixture nobody asked it about.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpiel`'s `team1`, `team2`, `elfmeterschiessen` and `sonderereignis`; what a save's resolution rewrote reaches the wire as `:: FLSpielAdvancement` and `:: FLSpielReleasedSide`.\
**Trap:** the word names which two clubs meet and the shape carries what they PRODUCED as well, so a resolution replacing an occupant voids the goals, the shoot-out record and a no-show with it. And it is not an `Aufstellung`: the site stores a starting line-up too, and that is the squad a club fields rather than the pairing it plays in.\
**See:** the `Quelle` entry below for what a standing reference owns.

### `Ansetzung` — the date, time, ground and two sides a fixture is set for

**Is:** what a `Spielbestätigung` answers about: a fixture's `datum`, `uhrzeit`, ground (`ort.spielort_id`) and two sides, and `stand`, a SHA-256 over those five. A change to any of the five is a new Ansetzung, and a row answering about the old one stops applying.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/services.py :: ANSETZUNG_QUELLEN`, `:: ansetzung_stand_of` and `:: compose_ansetzung`; stored on a row as `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/schemas.py :: FLSpielbestaetigungAnsetzung`; whether both clubs confirmed a fixture's current one is `ansetzung_bestaetigt`, declared on `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpielJoined` and joined by `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/services.py :: build_spiele_pipeline`.\
**Trap:** the ground is part of it, so moving only the venue reopens every confirmation of the fixture. The five values are stored beside the digest because no aggregation can recompute a SHA-256: a read joining rows compares the values while the answer compares the digest, and `ANSETZUNG_QUELLEN` is the one list both read, since a key added to one alone gives two answers to whether a row is about the fixture's current Ansetzung. `ansetzung_bestaetigt` has three values, and `null`, no club's row about the fixture standing unclosed nor one discarded about its current Ansetzung, is never `false`: only `false` puts „Vorläufig“ on a public card. A fixture moved since its clubs were asked reads `false` at once, from its rows about the Ansetzung it left, until the next pass closes them; it reads `false` again only once a pass asks about the new Ansetzung, which happens only inside the answer window (`fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/services.py :: letzter_anfrage_tag`) and on a fixture holding a date, a time, a ground and both sides, so a fixture moved further out, or left without one of those, reads `null` in between. A fixture whose day passed with no result reads `null` from the pass that closes its rows `spiel_vergangen`. It is neither the `Paarung`, the two sides and what they produced, nor the `Spieltag` a fixture belongs to.\
**See:** the `Spielbestätigung` entry, and the `Paarung` entry for the sides alone.

### `Einteilung` — the message telling a referee they are booked onto one fixture

**Is:** what a referee booked onto a fixture is told of it: once the confirmation sweep mints the referee's own `Spielbestätigung` row for the fixture's current `Ansetzung`, it mails them the Einteilung, naming the fixture, its slot and its ground and leading to their panel.\
**In code:** the referee's row is `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/schemas.py :: FLSpielbestaetigungParteiSchiedsrichter`; the pass (`fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/system_router.py :: sweep_spielbestaetigungen`) owes it on the Postausgang as `fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/schemas.py :: FLSpielEinteilungDaten`, the message is `fl_frontend/src/core/spielEmail.ts :: buildSpielEinteilungEmail`, and the Postausgang's drain sends it through `fl_frontend/src/core/mailKinds.ts :: MAIL_KINDS`.\
**Trap:** the booking itself is the fixture editor's write, never this word's: an Einteilung only reports one, once per referee and Ansetzung, so moving the fixture voids it through the „Neue Ansetzung“ notice and a fresh one follows only while the referee stays booked. It carries no token and leads to `/bereich/schiedsrichter`, which opens signed in, where the referee accepts or declines; a fixture is booked onto a referee only once their own record is confirmed (`booking_unconfirmed_schiedsrichter`), so the address it goes to holds the referee Funktion that page asks for. Its delivery is recorded on its Postausgang row, as every league mail's is. Besides the move's „Neue Ansetzung“, two notices void it, each under two names: a referee taken off the fixture is `schiedsrichter_gewechselt` on the wire and „Einteilung aufgehoben“ in the mail, and a call-off `schiedsrichter_spiel_abgesagt` and „Spiel abgesagt“.\
**See:** the `Spielbestätigung` and `Ansetzung` entries, and the `Schiedsrichter` entry for the booking the message reports.

### `Einsatz` — a fixture a referee is booked onto, as their own panel lists it

**Is:** one card of „Deine Einsätze“ on a referee's own page: the newest `Spielbestätigung` row about one fixture, with the Ansetzung it asked about, its state, whether the league entered its answer (`eingetragen`) and whether its day is past (`vergangen`).\
**In code:** served as `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/schemas.py :: FLSpielbestaetigungSelf` by `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/person_router.py :: get_self`, drawn by `fl_frontend/src/features/spielbestaetigungen/components/views/SchiedsrichterEinsaetzeSection.tsx`; whether a fixture's referee agreed is `einsatz_bestaetigt`, joined by `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/services.py :: build_spiele_pipeline`.\
**Trap:** one card per fixture, so a move, a reinstatement or a re-booking replaces the card with the fresh request rather than adding one. `eingetragen` says the league recorded the answer, and is not a seat's `eingetragen_von`, which says who seated a contact. `einsatz_bestaetigt` has two values where `ansetzung_bestaetigt` has three: a booking the pass has not asked about yet reads `false` and withholds the referee block as a silence or a decline does, and only `true`, their agreement or the league's entry of it, serves it.\
**See:** the `Einteilung` entry for the message leading to the card, and the `Absage` entry for the referee's decline on it.

### `vorschlag` — a date and hour a club proposes, and the link state showing the other club's

**Is:** on a `Spielbestätigung`'s answer, `antwort.vorschlag` is the date and hour a club's decline proposes instead of the Ansetzung it was asked about; as a link's state, `vorschlag` is what a still-answerable link shows while the OTHER club's open decline proposes another date for the same Ansetzung, served beside it.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/schemas.py :: FLSpielbestaetigungZustand` names the state and `:: FLSpielbestaetigungTermin` the date and hour; `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/services.py :: zustand_of` picks the state.\
**Trap:** the state never means that this link proposed anything: a club that declined reads `abgelehnt` on its own links, and the reader of `vorschlag` is the club still to answer, which may agree to the Ansetzung as it stands or decline with a date of its own. Neither reading says the fixture moved: a proposal moves nothing, and only the administrator's save of the date does, judged again.\
**See:** the `Spielbestätigung` and `Ansetzung` entries.

### `halter` — who answered a fixture confirmation from a seat, as a keyed digest of their address

**Is:** `antwort.von.halter` on a `Spielbestätigung`: a keyed digest of the answering seat holder's folded address, written with the answer and served by no read, so a row names who answered by ROLE alone while the person's erasure or Widerspruch can still find every reason they typed.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/services.py :: halter_of`, written through `:: compose_antwort_update` and matched by `:: build_contact_reasons_filter`.\
**Trap:** it follows the PERSON and never the seat: one digest across every seat and club they answered from, so a person's erasure reaches a reason typed from a seat they have since left, and the seat's next holder never reaches it. It is pseudonymised personal data rather than none, the key recomputing it from the address ([`datenschutz.md`](datenschutz.md#5-erasure-reaches-everyone-who-asks) §5), and it goes with its season's contacts (§6).\
**See:** the `Spielbestätigung` entry, and [ops runbooks §5](ops/runbooks.md#5-when-somebody-asks-for-their-data-or-asks-us-to-change-it) for finding a person's answers by it.

### `Zusage` — one word over two acts: a club agreeing to a fixture's date, and the league accepting a school

**Is:** on a fixture confirmation, a club's or a booked referee's agreement to the Ansetzung it was asked about, or the league's recorded for either, stored as `antwort.art` `zugesagt` and pressed as „Zusagen“; in the application triage, the league's acceptance of a `Bewerbung` and the message announcing it.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/schemas.py :: FLSpielbestaetigungAntwortArt` for the first, a referee's pressed on their own panel through `fl_backend/app/api/spielbestaetigungen/person_router.py :: post_schiedsrichter_antwort`; for the second the decision is `POST /bewerbungen/{bewerbung_id}/annehmen` and its message `fl_frontend/src/core/bewerbungEmail.ts :: buildBewerbungZusageEmail`.\
**Trap:** the two share the word and nothing else. A club's Zusage is whichever seat presses first, or an administrator's recorded for the club with a reason and naming nobody, binds the club rather than the person pressing, and takes no second answer, only a move of the fixture asking afresh; a referee's is their own, given signed in until the fixture's day, or an administrator's recorded for them with a reason, which counts alike; the league's is an administrator's irreversible decision entering a school into a season. A name, a facet or a sentence taking one for the other lands in the wrong slice, and neither is a `Bestätigung`, a person's own answer about their own record.\
**See:** the `Spielbestätigung` and `Bewerbung` entries, and the `Bestätigung` entry for the word neither is.

### `Absage` — one word over two acts: the league refusing an application, and a referee declining a booking

**Is:** in the application and registration triage, the league's refusal and the message announcing it; on a referee's own panel, their decline of a fixture they are booked onto, pressed as „Absagen“ and sent with its reason as „Absage senden“, stored as `antwort.art` `abgelehnt`.\
**In code:** `fl_frontend/src/core/bewerbungEmail.ts :: buildBewerbungAbsageEmail` and `fl_frontend/src/core/registrierungEmail.ts :: buildRegistrierungAbsageEmail` for the first; `fl_frontend/src/features/spielbestaetigungen/personActions.ts :: absagenSpielbestaetigungAction` for the second.\
**Trap:** `abgesagt` is neither act: it is a fixture called off, the status chip's „Abgesagt“ and a confirmation row's closing reason, which the referee's panel words „Spiel abgesagt“ beside their own decline's „Abgesagt“. A club's decline on a confirmation is no Absage either: every decline, a club's or a referee's, reads „Abgelehnt“ on the administrator's chips, because „Abgesagt“ stands for the call-off on the same card.\
**See:** the `Zusage` entry for the answer it pairs with, and the `Spielbestätigung` entry for the row a referee's decline closes.

### `Quelle` — where a side of a fixture comes from

**Is:** `team1_quelle` and `team2_quelle` — a structural reference naming what feeds that side of the bracket, a group placing or a match outcome, never display text.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpielQuelle`, resolved by `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/services.py :: resolve_bracket`, rendered by `fl_frontend/src/features/spiele/utils.ts :: formatQuelle`.\
**Trap:** it is not paired with the team field beside it — all four combinations are legitimate stored states — and while a reference stands it owns the slot, so a hand-set team is a 409 and is reverted on the next save. Clearing the reference is the only manual override.\
**See:** the `Herkunft` entry below for the German the editor labels this with and the three other things that word names.

### `Platz` — a placing in a group's standing

**Is:** `platz` inside a `gruppe` reference — `1` the group winner, `2` the runner-up; an `int` with `gt=0` and no upper bound.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpielQuelle`.\
**Trap:** it counts only teams that can hold a placing, so a team that has left the season keeps its table row while the place falls to the team below, and a team with no match that counts or still could holds no placing at all; a `platz` the group can never produce is reported rather than refused.

### `Ausgang` — which side of a match a reference names

**Is:** `ausgang` inside a `spiel` reference: `sieger` or `verlierer`.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpielQuelle`.\
**Trap:** `verlierer` exists because a third-place play-off is fed by two losing semi-finals, and nothing writes it in this season's bracket; a level match with no shoot-out has neither outcome, so a reference to either resolves to nobody and the slot stays empty.

### `Herkunft` — one German word over four unrelated origins

**Is:** the German a reader meets wherever something has a provenance, over four things that share nothing but the word: what feeds a side of a knockout fixture, which category of actor made a recorded write, whether an application names a new school or a club already in the league, and who seated a contact person.\
**In code:** a fixture side is `fl_frontend/src/features/spiele/utils.ts :: FLSlotHerkunft` over `:: deriveSlotHerkunft`, labelled by `fl_frontend/src/features/spiele/components/forms/AdminEditSpielDataForm/FormTeamPicker.tsx` and named in the draft's rows by `fl_frontend/src/features/spiele/draftStatus.ts`; the log's actor is `fl_backend/app/api/aktionen/schemas.py :: FLAktionHerkunft` over `:: HERKUNFT_JE_KIND`, mirrored by `fl_frontend/src/features/aktionen/constants.ts :: AktionHerkunft` and `:: AKTOR_HERKUNFT` and faceted by `fl_frontend/src/features/aktionen/facets.ts`; an application's is `fl_frontend/src/features/bewerbungen/utils.ts :: bewerbungHerkunft`, named by `fl_frontend/src/features/bewerbungen/constants.ts :: BEWERBUNG_HERKUNFT_LABELS` and read by the `herkunft` facet in `fl_frontend/src/features/bewerbungen/facets.ts`; a record's provenance is `fl_frontend/src/features/teams/constants.ts :: EINGETRAGEN_VON_OPTIONS` with `:: eingetragenVonLabel` for who seated a contact person, on an application and a team's seat alike.\
**Trap:** no page shows two of the four, so the word reads as one thing until a helper, a facet, a type or a label is written across two — and three of the four are on no stored field at all. The log's is the category `HERKUNFT_JE_KIND` files `actor.kind` under rather than anything a row carries; an application's is derived from which of `schule` and `team_id` holds a value, so a row holding neither matches no option instead of being filed under one; a slot's is derived from the `team` and `quelle` pair, and `FLSlotHerkunft` is WIDER than the reference it labels, adding `manuell` and `offen` for the two states no `Quelle` can express. Only the provenance sense is stored, and never under this name: `eingetragen_von` on a contact seat says who seated its person. The two counted senses are counted differently — the log's come from the server and the application queue's from the loaded rows alone — so a zero means different things on the two pages. And a grep for the word finds a fifth use with no German behind it, the cross-origin refusal `fl_frontend/src/shared/utils/publicRoute.ts :: FREMDE_HERKUNFT`, which is a REQUEST's origin and reaches no reader's screen as this word at all.\
**See:** the `Quelle` entry above for the stored reference the fixture sense labels and the `Einwilligung` · `Kenntnisnahme` entry below for the two provenance vocabularies; the `Aktion` entry for the write the log's sense categorises.

### `austritt` — out of one season, by which route and from when

**Is:** a record on the `saison_teams` junction carrying `type`, `grund` and `datum`, so a team is out of one season rather than out of the league, and the record says which of the two routes out it took — `disqualifikation` or `rueckzug`.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLAustritt`, joined from the junction; the German for each route is `fl_frontend/src/features/teams/constants.ts :: AUSTRITT_OPTIONS`, which every surface reads rather than writing its own.\
**Trap:** its absence is the null and no boolean records the same fact anywhere; replacing the club clears the record, which left standing would mark the INCOMING club withdrawn; no rule keyed on a club having left reads `type`, so a withdrawal keeps a club off a later fixture exactly as a sanction does — what a rule reads is the record's presence (`fl_backend/app/api/teams/services.py :: _may_hold_a_platz`) or its `datum` (`fl_backend/app/api/spiele/services.py :: find_departed_occupants`), and `type` reaches a reader only as something reported; `grund` is public and rendered as authored (`READ-FREETEXT-002`); and `GET /teams?has_austritt=` is turned into a null test on the whole record, so it selects on having left by either route, `austritt_type=` beside it being what narrows to one of them — two independent terms rather than one nested under the other, so naming a route needs no boolean with it.

### `ist_nachnominiert` — entered later, retrospectively added

**Is:** a marker on a squad entry made on or after the first day of the season's matchday 1.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spieler/schemas.py :: FLSaisonSpielerRow`, the `saison_spieler` row's own declared shape.\
**Trap:** the squad create and the admission derive it from matchday 1 of the season's FIRST phase (`fl_backend/app/api/spieltage/crud.py :: nachnominierung_laeuft_in`), never from the season's status: an `active` season whose matchday 1 is undated or still ahead enters players as ordinary ones, and the invite's read tells a pupil the same verdict; no payload carries it, so no form and no edit can set or clear it, and an admission reviving a retired row derives it afresh.

### `rolle` — which of a squad's leading roles a player holds

**Is:** the Kapitän or the Co-Kapitän of one team for one season, held on the `saison_spieler` junction rather than on the person. ONE nullable closed set rather than a flag per role, so holding both at once is unrepresentable rather than refused, and null — holding neither — is the ordinary state.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spieler/schemas.py :: FLSpielerRolle`, on `:: FLSaisonSpielerRow` beside `ist_nachnominiert`; the German for each value is `fl_frontend/src/features/spieler/constants.ts :: ROLLE_OPTIONS`, which every surface reads rather than spelling its own.\
**Trap:** a squad holds each role at most once among its LIVE rows and every squad write path refuses one already held (`kader_rolle_taken`), so a role is handed on by taking it off its current holder or retiring them; and `fl_backend/app/shared/schemas/custom.py :: PERSON_NAME_PATTERN` on the write payloads is what stops the marker being typed inside a name instead.

### `verwaltung` — which tier of access to the league's administration a grant holds

**Is:** `owner` or `administration`, a closed set on the grant row and the one answer `POST /identitaet/subjekt` gives beside the derived records, null there for an address holding no grant. `owner` holds every power `administration` holds, is out of an administrator's reach, and alone revokes a grant and changes a tier. On screen `owner` is „Inhaberin oder Inhaber“, the badge and every sentence alike, `administration` has no badge and is „Verwaltung“ only in the tier change's labels, each built on „zur Verwaltung herabstufen“ whether the row is another's or one's own and resting or armed (`fl_frontend/src/features/berechtigungen/constants.ts :: stufeWorte`), and a grant's tier is its „Stufe“.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/berechtigungen/schemas.py :: FLVerwaltung`, served as `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/schemas.py :: FLSubjektResponse`'s `verwaltung`, and moved by the tier change `fl_backend/app/api/berechtigungen/admin_router.py :: patch_berechtigung`, whose payload is `fl_backend/app/api/berechtigungen/schemas.py :: FLPatchBerechtigungPayload`.\
**Trap:** only an owner makes an `owner`, by a tier change, and only an erasure or an address change moving it to its holder's new address removes one, never a revoke: an owner is made an administrator first, and the last owner is demoted by nobody and erases themselves only once another is made. A promotion holds for a sign-in after it alone, dated as `inhaber_seit` by the tier change or, for one written in the database, by the reconciliation's find, so a session older than it administers and holds no owner's power; a demotion takes the tier at once. The database is written directly only for the first owner and for recovery when no owner can sign in. The word is German for the whole administration, so a name built from it reads as the admin area rather than a tier; the tier is this field and nothing else. The screen spends the word both ways, the area in „Zugang zur Verwaltung“, „Inhaberin oder Inhaber der Verwaltung“ and the admin area's own name „Verwaltung“ and the lower tier in those labels, so a sentence naming the tier by „Verwaltung“ alone reads as the area. It is neither `rolle`, the captaincy and the contact seat, nor `role`, the frontend session's administrator verdict; and its „Stufe“ on screen is never the squad row's `stufe`.\
**See:** the `Berechtigung` entry for the row.

### `stufe` — which Oberstufe level a squad row records

**Is:** the Halbjahr of the Hessen Oberstufe a player is in, a closed set covering both phases, held on the `saison_spieler` junction and nullable, with each season declaring in its rules which of the members its squad form offers. Inside `oberstufengymnasium` the word is not this field at all, that being a `schulform` value describing the SCHOOL rather than anybody in it, and inside `stufengroesse` it is a whole Abi-Jahrgang rather than a level.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spieler/schemas.py :: FLSpielerStufe`, declared on `:: _SaisonSpielerWritable`, which the squad payloads and the flattened read share, and again on `:: FLSaisonSpielerRow`; the season's narrowing is `fl_backend/app/api/saisons/schemas.py :: FLSaisonRules.erlaubte_stufen`, whose list every picker orders against `fl_frontend/src/features/spieler/constants.ts :: orderStufen`.\
**Trap:** `erlaubte_stufen` bounds what the squad FORM offers and never what a stored row holds, so narrowing it leaves the rows outside it readable rather than invalid, while a pupil not yet admitted is refused at it, at the submission and again at the admission (`registrierung_stufe_nicht_erlaubt`); `E2` is in the closed set although the current season has nobody in it, a set stopping at what a season contains refusing an entry as the year turns; and the base tier is served no `stufe` at all (`READ-PUPIL-002`), so a public squad list carries none; and it is not an age — the person's own `geburtsdatum` answers that, and no squad rule judges it ([`domain.md`](domain.md#what-the-model-permits-on-purpose)).\
**See:** [`domain.md`](domain.md) for why a substring match attributes this term's facts to another concept.

### `stufengroesse` — how many pupils are in the Abi-Jahrgang the team comes from

**Is:** one number on a public application: the whole Abi-Jahrgang the team comes from, the pupils who will never play included, rather than the squad it expects to field. Stated once by the school and checked against nothing afterwards — no season rule bounds it, and nothing compares it to the squad that arrives at acceptance.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbung.stufengroesse`, required and bounded on the write alone by `:: FLPostBewerbungPayload` against `fl_backend/app/shared/schemas/bounds.py :: BEWERBUNG_STUFENGROESSE_MAX`; the stored shape is `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbungRecord`; the label `Größe der Stufe` and the sentence under it that fixes which pupils are counted are `fl_frontend/src/features/bewerbungen/components/forms/BewerbungForm/FormSchuleSection.tsx`.\
**Trap:** the name carries `stufe` and the number counts no `stufe` — a `stufe` is one Halbjahr and an Abi-Jahrgang runs through all six of them, so no single `stufe` ever names the cohort this field counts, and `erlaubte_stufen` answers a different question again; the label alone says `Stufe` and the sentence under it is what settles the cohort, so prose written from the label counts the wrong pupils; and the ceiling is the write payload's alone, a stored value over it still reading rather than answering 500 for a whole triage list.\
**See:** [`domain.md`](domain.md) for why a substring match attributes this term's facts to another concept.

### `wunschgegner` — the opponent a school wishes to meet on the first Spieltag

**Is:** a school's free-text wish on its application, naming the side it would like to play first, or null where it named nobody. A wish recorded and repeated back, never a fixture: the Spielplan decides the pairings.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbung.wunschgegner`, trimmed and bounded on the write by `:: FLPostBewerbungPayload` against `fl_backend/app/shared/schemas/bounds.py :: BEWERBUNG_WUNSCHGEGNER_MAX_LENGTH`; the acceptance mail states it from its Postausgang row's `fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/schemas.py :: FLBewerbungZusageDaten`, where the field has no default so no writer can leave it out.\
**Trap:** a string, never a club reference — a school may name an applicant the league has not accepted yet, so no reading of it resolves to a `Team`, and nothing ever pairs on it; the acceptance mail says the draw decides for the same reason. An empty or blank entry is stored as null rather than as a wish, and the write payload defaults the key where its siblings require theirs, so a form that stops sending it ships a silent null.\
**See:** the `Spielplan` entry above for what decides the pairings.

### `Kürzel` · `shorthand` — the three letters one club is known by

**Is:** the code a club is identified by league-wide, unique across every club and retired ones with them. A school proposes one on its application before any club exists to hold it, and the acceptance is what turns the proposal into a club's own.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/shared/schemas/custom.py :: CustomShorthand` strips and upper-cases the code and holds it to `:: SHORTHAND_PATTERN`, the width `fl_backend/app/shared/schemas/bounds.py :: TEAM_SHORTHAND_LENGTH` fixes, which the published document carries as each field's `pattern`, and `fl_frontend/src/features/bewerbungen/constants.ts :: KUERZEL_LAENGE` mirrors it; `fl_backend/app/core/constraints.py :: uniq_shorthand` holds the club's copy unique; the availability read is `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/public_router.py :: get_kuerzel`, answering `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbungKuerzelResponse`.\
**Trap:** the German and the English meet inside one URL, `/bewerbungen/kuerzel/{shorthand}`, so a search for either spelling finds none of the other's sites. Uniqueness is byte for byte, and holds case-blind only because every model taking a code folds it first; a row written outside the application keeps the case it was typed in, which is why the triage's `Dublette` folds case where the index does not. A season read serves the JUNCTION's copy, rewritten only into seasons that are not `past`, so a club recoded after a season finished still reads there under the code it played under and what is on screen is no key to look the club up by; `GET /teams/saisons` serves the club's own instead. At the unauthenticated write a code some club already holds is refused on the box that holds it (`bewerbung_shorthand_taken`) and the availability read narrows that collision without closing it, while two applications proposing one FREE code are both accepted — uniqueness across applications would let a stranger lock a school out by typing its code — so the queue marks the pair a `Dublette` and the SECOND acceptance is the one `uniq_shorthand` refuses.\
**See:** the `Team` entry above for the club this identifies, and the `Dublette` entry below for the fold the triage applies.

### `schulform` — which kind of school a club is

**Is:** a closed set of school-kind slugs, or null where nobody has recorded it. G8 and G9 are told apart because they are two school careers rather than two names for one building.\

A club's NAME settles at most half of it — `Gymnasium` says nothing about whether that school runs G8 or G9 — so what cannot be derived from a name is left null for a person to set in the editor rather than guessed.\
**See:** [`ops/runbooks.md`](ops/runbooks.md) §2 for the order a change to a stored shape or an index runs in.

### `trikot_farbe` — the kit colour a club plays a season in

**Is:** one of the league's own colours as a slug, held on the `saison_teams` junction and assigned by an administrator, or null before one is.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLTrikotFarbe`, declared on the junction's record `:: FLSaisonTeamRecord` and on `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbungTrikot` for the wish; the German name and the hex for each value are `fl_frontend/src/features/teams/constants.ts :: TRIKOT_FARBE_OPTIONS`.\
**Trap:** an application's `trikot.wunschfarbe` is the same closed set in a different role — what a school ASKED for — and nothing copies it across: acceptance takes the colour it assigns from its own payload, because a wish is not an assignment and two schools may wish for one colour. The wish is required on every application, so a stored one always names a colour, while the junction's colour stays null until an administrator assigns one. Which colours a season has already ASSIGNED is readable from the public tier through `GET /bewerbungen/trikotfarben/{saison_id}`, so a form can offer the rest — off the junction, never off another application's wish (`READ-BEWERBUNG-001`). The vocabulary is the league's colour sheet's, not a form's — `Grün` and `Magenta`, never `Dunkelgrün` or `Pink`. The hex beside a slug is drawing data and is stored nowhere, so nothing reads a colour back as a value, and changing one recolours every row already stored under that slug and contradicts every acceptance already mailed, which states the hex to the school.\
**See:** [`backend/overview.md`](backend/overview.md) for why this junction's fields are transcribed by hand.

### `kontakte` — the three people the league reaches a team through

**Is:** a block of three slots — an Ansprechperson, a Stellvertretung and a Trainer — beside a declaration naming which OTHER seat the Trainer also holds, or nobody, held on TWO collections from one pair of declarations: nullable as a whole on a `saison_teams` row, which is entered before anybody has been recorded, and required on an application, which IS the form those people filled in.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLSaisonTeamKontakte` over `:: FLKontaktperson` and `:: FLKontaktKenntnisnahme`, tightened for a write by `:: FLSaisonTeamKontaktePayload` and imported rather than restated by `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbung`. A junction row's three are entered on a page of their own, `fl_frontend/src/features/kontakte/components/forms/AdminKontakteEditForm/FormKontakteSection.tsx`, which the club's season panel links to rather than holding a field of; an accepted application's arrive with the row acceptance writes, and an erasure of a person's address is what takes them out of both collections (`fl_backend/app/api/kontakte/crud.py :: loeschen_kontaktperson_in_session`).\
**Trap:** each slot is nullable on its own on the stored shape and on the JUNCTION's write — an application's payload requires all three, non-null (`fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbungKontaktePayload`) — so an erasure can empty the slot naming one person without reaching the two beside them, and a row it emptied still saves — a payload requiring all three would have made every later edit to that row re-collect the person who asked to be forgotten. On a junction row nothing guarantees three people: `FormKontakteSection.tsx` opens a first fill with all three seats on, and any seat switched off saves as `null`; on an application three whole people are the payload's requirement, and `trainer_ist_zugleich` stands through an erasure, recording what somebody asserted rather than what the block now holds. Season-scoped and never carried forward: these are one cohort's people, so a new season collects them again rather than inheriting them, and a replacement clears the block rather than handing the outgoing school's people to the incoming one. `FLKontaktKenntnisnahme` is deliberately not the `einwilligung` a pupil carries: that one is the person's consent to what is PUBLISHED about them, this one an acknowledgement that these details are held and used, whose only consents are the optional WhatsApp scope and `medien`, and entangling them would put a club's contacts behind an open question about pupil data. A seat's `geburtsdatum` is null until its person enters it at the confirmation, and no payload asks for it — `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbungKontaktpersonPayload` and the junction editor's `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLKontaktpersonPayload` both refuse the key outright rather than taking a null, so a junction save carries over whatever date the row already holds for the person being written — the same address and the same name, folded. `bestaetigt_am` is the server's on every write path and no write names who answered, and the junction editor keeps a seat's stored record whole, its choices and their evidence included, wherever that same person still holds it, confirmed or not, while a seat newly filled or handed to another person is born unconfirmed, granting nothing and with no evidence, so a corrected typo in a name costs its person a fresh confirmation. The junction join withholds this block from the base tier inside the `$lookup` rather than at a later stage, a club's public read being one aggregation away from it otherwise.\
**See:** [`backend/overview.md`](backend/overview.md) for the junction having no stored-document model, and [`domain.md`](domain.md) for where a season-scoped fact belongs.

### `Einwilligung` · `Kenntnisnahme` — one stored key over two vocabularies: a person's own consent, and what a contact seat was told

**Is:** the block stored under the key `einwilligung` saying what it covers (`umfang`), when (`datum`), and the day it was confirmed (`bestaetigt_am`, null while it is not); carried once on a pupil, once on a referee, once on a pending registration and once on each contact seat, on a team's season row and on an application alike, each citing the wording that person was shown (`text_version`), each carrying a consent to photographs, video and interviews (`medien`), independent of `umfang`, and each keeping under `nachweis`, per choice, the instant in UTC its person last set it (`am`) and the label it was set under, a withdrawal also naming the grant it ended (`erteilt_zuvor`), a returning pupil's registration excepted, which holds the stamp and the label alone; a contact seat also names who seated its person (`eingetragen_von`: `bewerbung` for the applicant, `liga` for the league's administration).\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spieler/schemas.py :: FLEinwilligung` for a pupil and a referee, imported rather than restated by `fl_backend/app/api/schiedsrichter/schemas.py :: FLSchiedsrichter`, its subclass `fl_backend/app/api/registrierungen/schemas.py :: FLRegistrierungEinwilligung` for a pending registration, whose choices are optional, and `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLKontaktKenntnisnahme` for a contact seat; the evidence is `fl_backend/app/shared/schemas/einwilligung.py :: FLEinwilligungNachweise` on both, composed by `fl_backend/app/shared/einwilligung_nachweis.py` alone; the frontend reads each half as the generated `fl_frontend/src/core/generated/client/types.ts :: FLEinwilligung` and `:: FLKontaktKenntnisnahme`, and `fl_backend/app/shared/einwilligung.py :: FASSUNGEN` is the registry every `text_version` names an entry of, which the frontend reads through `fl_frontend/src/core/einwilligung.ts :: getEinwilligungFassung` and never copies.\
**Trap:** a contact seat's block is no consent and the stored key says otherwise — `einwilligung` on both collections, so a grep for the key finds both vocabularies and a rename of it would move every stored record. What says which vocabulary a record is in is the model name, and, to the person, the notice, which calls a contact seat's answer eine Bestätigung and not eine Einwilligung (`fl_frontend/src/features/meta/components/views/DatenschutzView.tsx`), and the confirmation page, whose paragraphs call it the same (`fl_backend/app/shared/einwilligung.py :: FASSUNGEN`). What the seat's data rests on is legitimate interest under Art. 6(1)(f), consent being kept for what is published ([`datenschutz.md`](datenschutz.md#2-consent-comes-from-the-person-from-16-or-18)), and the consents on that block are the optional WhatsApp scope and `medien`. **The German a reader meets is two words for two acts of that one block, and whose act it was decides which.** `Kenntnisnahme` is the record standing on nobody's own answer — the applicant's blanket acknowledgement for three people on the public form (`fl_frontend/src/features/bewerbungen/components/forms/BewerbungForm/FormKontaktpersonenSection.tsx`), the junction editor's administrative one, its heading and its facet (`fl_frontend/src/features/kontakte/components/forms/AdminKontakteEditForm/FormKontakteSection.tsx`, `fl_frontend/src/features/teams/facets.ts`), and every Zod message about the block — while a seat person's own answer to their own link is a `Bestätigung`, which is what the mails, the confirmation page and the notice call it. A heading or a sentence taking one word for the other tells the reader the wrong thing about who answered. The two models share every field name but the provenance one, and not one value. `umfang` is `kader_oeffentlich` or `intern` on a pupil and `kontaktdaten` or `kontaktdaten_whatsapp` on a contact seat, and on a pupil it is not the whole answer — `medien` is a second consent on the same block, so whether a photograph, a video or an interview may be published is that field's answer and never `umfang`'s, withdrawing one leaves the other standing, and a `true` there is taken only from a person of `fl_backend/app/shared/schemas/bounds.py :: MEDIEN_MIN_AGE_YEARS`, a floor over every age span a seat or a role allows, so on a younger person's record `false` means the switch was never offered rather than declined; whether a seat's person answered is `bestaetigt_am`'s to say and who seated them `eingetragen_von`'s. The league has no ONE minimum age for a seat's person: the floor is per seat (`fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/services.py :: SEAT_MIN_AGE_YEARS`), and a person's is the highest of the seats they hold rather than the one they are answering for. A helper, a facet or a label written against one model is therefore wrong on the other, and no type refuses the reuse. `text_version` names a REGISTRY LABEL and never the words: it says which text the stamping person was shown, so an entry there is never reworded, nor removed while a stored record can cite it, and new wording is a new label — which is why the confirmation page carries one of its own, the applicant who ticked for three people having read a different page, and why a person an administrator seated reads a confirmation page of their own, one on an application and one on a team's season row, saying who entered them. The WhatsApp scope is on no administrative payload, so no administrative write can transcribe it: its person moves it on the account page, a past season's and a pending application's seat taking its withdrawal alone. And `bestaetigt_am` on a contact seat is stamped by that seat's own link and by nothing else, while on a pupil it and `text_version` name the latest confirmation that asked the pupil's choices: the admission carries both from a new pupil's registration confirmed no earlier than the record, and a returning pupil's, which asked none, moves neither. A referee's record and a pending registration's are the two nobody but their own person writes: both sit on no administrative payload and are composed by that person's own confirmation, and afterwards a referee's two choices move through that person's own account page alone, a pupil's through it and through a later season's admission, which renews each choice the pupil's own registration set later, and a pending registration's are withdrawn there alone until its admission, so no administrative save puts one there or corrects one, and a registration's arrives with the birthdate in a single `$set`. **`nachweis` sits INSIDE the block, so a write `$set`ting an existing block whole erases the proof of every choice on it**: a block that exists moves field by field. Only a person's own answer grants a choice or stamps its evidence in a write the code makes — their own write, or an admission carrying their own registration's answer; an administrative save writes the acknowledgement with media off, and keeps a kept person's choices as stored, so the evidence names no speaker; the one evidence written outside the code sits on a contact seat's WhatsApp grant older than seats' evidence, copied by a hand paste from its confirmation's log row ([`datenschutz.md`](datenschutz.md#11-open-and-owed-a-decision)). Its `text_version` is the label that act was given under, so a press on the account page records its control's label there and leaves the block's, the label its person confirmed. It keeps one act per choice and never a history: an earlier act leaves only the action log's row, and one from before the log was emptied in full leaves nothing. A Widerspruch is no act at all — it empties the slot, its record being `abgelehnt_am` beside it. And `eingetragen_von` is never `verwaltung`, the access tier's word (the `verwaltung` entry above): it is written once by the seating and moved by nothing, since it decides which confirmation page the seat's link opens.\
**See:** the `kontakte` entry above for why the two vocabularies stay unshared, and [`datenschutz.md`](datenschutz.md#2-consent-comes-from-the-person-from-16-or-18) for the rulings the contact half implements.

### `Bestätigung` — one person's own answer to their own link, and the bookkeeping beside it on a seat, a registration or a referee

**Is:** the bookkeeping beside each seat of a `Bewerbung` — the hash of the link that seat's person was mailed, the day it went, the day a reminder went and the day they exercised their `Widerspruch` (`abgelehnt_am`) — kept beside the slot rather than inside it, so a Widerspruch that empties the person's slot leaves the record of it standing. One link opens two doors: the person confirms their entry, or contradicts it, and the second empties the slot and, on an application, tells the submitter. The same bookkeeping sits on a pending `Registrierung`, on a `Schiedsrichter` row and beside each seat of a team's season row an administrator filled, each minted by that person's own link and each carrying its own `frist` beside the hash and the days. A change of a person's address keeps its links' hashes in a record of its own (the `Adresswechsel` entry below): its link goes to the new address and confirms only that the mailbox is theirs.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbungBestaetigungen` over `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbungBestaetigung`, stored with its hashes as `:: FLBewerbungBestaetigungRecord`; written by `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/services.py :: compose_bestaetigung` at submission, `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/services.py :: compose_erinnerung_update` for a reminder and `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/services.py :: compose_erneut_update` for an administrator's re-send; the link opens `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/public_router.py`, and `fl_frontend/src/features/bewerbungen/components/views/BestaetigungView.tsx` is the page behind it. The other three blocks are spelled apart from it rather than shared, `fl_backend/app/api/registrierungen/schemas.py :: FLRegistrierungBestaetigungRecord`, `fl_backend/app/api/schiedsrichter/schemas.py :: FLSchiedsrichterBestaetigungRecord` and `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLSaisonTeamBestaetigungRecord`, minted by `fl_backend/app/api/registrierungen/services.py :: compose_bestaetigung`, `fl_backend/app/api/schiedsrichter/services.py :: compose_bestaetigung` and `fl_backend/app/api/teams/services.py :: compose_kontakt_bestaetigung`, the last by the contacts save for each person it newly seats and by `fl_backend/app/api/teams/admin_router.py :: einladen_kontakt`.\
**Trap:** the raw token is the whole credential and exists only in the mail — the document keeps the hash of every live link in the seat's `token_hashes`, minted there when the mail's hand-out leaves the Postausgang (`fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/system_router.py :: post_ausgabe`) and never by the write that owes the mail, and no read model declares the array, so no admin read can serve one and a lost link is re-minted rather than recovered. A REMINDER and a RE-SEND are different acts: the reminder mints a fresh link and keeps the first hash live, so both messages work and `bestaetigungsfrist` does not move, while a re-send replaces the whole entry of every seat that person holds, which is what voids the earlier links and restarts the deadline. A submission's REPLAY is no act at all, on an application and on a registration alike: the first press owed every mail in its own write, keyed by the record and its person, so the replay answers the stored record and owes nothing again. That deadline sits on the APPLICATION and not on the seat, so one seat's re-send moves it for all three, and a deadline that passes deletes the whole application with every contact record on it. And the pending state has no member of `status`: an application nobody has confirmed reads `eingereicht` like any other, and it is acceptance that refuses while a seat is outstanding (`bewerbung_kontakte_unconfirmed`). Outstanding is read INSIDE the slot and never from this block: `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/services.py :: ausstehende_seats` asks each seat's `Kenntnisnahme` for its `bestaetigt_am`, so the day a link went, a reminder's day and an `abgelehnt_am` recorded here move acceptance not at all — which is why a Widerspruch counts as answered to the link that spends it (`fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/services.py :: seat_is_answered`) and stays outstanding to the acceptance writing `saison_teams`, one seat read by two questions. The second door is spelled two ways: the screen and every message call it Widerspruch (`fl_frontend/src/features/bewerbungen/components/views/BestaetigungFormPanel.tsx :: WIDERSPRUCH_SENDEN`) while the wire and the stamp say `abgelehnt`, so a sentence, a facet or a label taking the schema's word for the reader's puts a rejection in the mouth of the person who was entered rather than the league's — `Absage` there being the league's own refusal of a whole application (the `Absage` entry). **The word is that door's alone**: the Art. 21 DSGVO objection a confirmation page states is another right, so its paragraph is keyed `art21` in each page's map (`fl_frontend/src/core/einwilligungSeiten.ts :: KONTAKT_ABSATZ_SCHLUESSEL`, `:: SPIELER_ABSATZ_SCHLUESSEL` and `:: SCHIEDSRICHTER_ABSATZ_SCHLUESSEL`), and a key spelling `widerspruch` sends the next reader looking for the decline to the right to object. **The DEADLINE is not in one place.** A registration's and a referee's `frist` sit inside that row's own block, and a season row's beside each seat, stamped at the mint rather than derived, so raising either bound moves no deadline already in somebody's inbox, and a reader taking the application's shape for the rule looks for a registration's deadline in a field that does not exist. **The second door is the seat's alone:** a registration's block carries no `abgelehnt_am` and its page offers no Widerspruch, so a pupil who does not want the entry lets the link lapse and the sweep erases the row; and a season row's seat has the door but nobody to tell, an administrator rather than a submitter having entered its person, so its Widerspruch empties the slot and mails no one. A referee's block carries no such member, and nothing chases a referee's link, so the link a re-send or an UNCONFIRMED referee's address correction owes replaces every hash the block holds when it is handed out, and the link before it stops working then, where a seat's reminder and a registration's leave the first link live; a season row's seat has no reminder chasing its link either, and its re-send's link replaces the seat's hashes as a referee's does. **An address link confirms a mailbox and nothing else**: the change it confirms moves the person's records and leaves every consent record as it stood, and until it takes effect no read of a person's own records or of the sign-in keys on the address it holds, so taking that link for a consent link, or keying a read on its address, hands the person's records to whoever holds a mistyped address.\
**See:** the `Einwilligung` entry above for what a confirmation stamps inside the slot, the `Zustellstand` entry for the delivery state, which sits on the seat's newest Postausgang row rather than in this block, the editors joining it in on read — and the `Bewerbung` entry for the application this hangs off. Widerspruch has no entry of its own: it is one of this term's two doors, and splitting it out would put a seat's two answers on two pages.

### `Adresswechsel` · `adresswechsel` — a change of the address a person signs in on, and the record it waits in

**Is:** a change of the address every record of one person is keyed on, on screen „Änderung“ of the „E-Mail-Adresse“. It waits as one record of its own, keyed by the address on file, and taking effect moves every record of that address at once: pupil row, confirmed referee row, confirmed seats, registrations and a grant. It starts in one of three ways, its `weg`: on the person's own account page (`konto`); by an administrator, the address on file approving it first, its „Freigabe“ (`freigabe`); or by an administrator with a recorded reason, effect following the confirmation `fl_backend/app/shared/schemas/bounds.py :: ADRESSWECHSEL_WARTEZEIT_TAGE` days later, its „Wartezeit“ (`wartezeit`). A change moving a grant also waits on another administrator's „Genehmigung“. The new mailbox confirms by a link; the address on file can „stoppen“ it by a link of its own or on its account page.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/collections.py :: Collection`'s `ADRESSWECHSEL` member, whose ends owe their notices to the Postausgang in the end's own transaction and whose effects owe an account's carry as `umzug`; `fl_backend/app/api/adresswechsel/services.py :: compose_record` writes the record, `fl_backend/app/api/adresswechsel/crud.py :: read_the_bestand` reads the records a change moves and `:: move_the_address_in_session` moves them; the account page's routes are `fl_backend/app/api/konto/person_router.py`, the links' `fl_backend/app/api/adresswechsel/public_router.py`, the administrators' tool `fl_backend/app/api/adresswechsel/admin_router.py`, and the hourly pass `fl_backend/app/api/adresswechsel/system_router.py :: sweep_adresswechsel`.\
**Trap:** an ended change is kept `fl_backend/app/shared/schemas/bounds.py :: ADRESSWECHSEL_NACHLAUF_TAGE` days with its addresses, reason and approval gone and its link hashes kept (`token_hashes`, `stopp.token_hashes`), except that an effect keeps its two addresses' keyed check values as `umgezogen`, so an erasure at the new address reaches the mail the replaced one holds, and, unless the change waited (`wartezeit`), both addresses as `umzug` until the account's carry is done. So most records hold a null `adresse`, and a read of a live change that keys on the address without its `$type` term meets the tombstones and loses the unique index. `token_hashes` is empty while the change waits on the address on file's approval, so an empty list is no link. One address holds one live change, a start replacing the one before. The change records which address it moves and never which records: a record written at the address during the wait moves too.\
**See:** the `Antrag` entry for the requests an effect ends, the `Bestätigung` entry above for the blocks beside a record, and the `Konto` entry below for the page.

### `Fassung` · `Seite` — one frozen wording a person was shown, and the page showing the running one

**Is:** a `Fassung` is one wording — its paragraphs, its switch and every other control that decides a stored field — under the label a consent or acknowledgement record stamps as `text_version`; a `Seite` is a page that shows one, from a closed set of the application form, the confirmation pages and the account page's controls, and each carries one running label that a new answer on it must name. **Trap:** a label never changes while a page's running label moves with every deploy that rewords it: new words are a new label beside the old, never an edit of one, because every stored record cites its words by label. So a page loaded before such a deploy names a label its own write then refuses, a reader caching the running labels refuses its visitors' presses until the cache expires, and an older label of the page is admitted only from a withdrawal, taking a consent back never being harder than giving it, and from a contacts save naming back the label a kept person already holds. And a `Seite` is the registry's key, not a route a visitor opens and not the „Seite“ a reload message means: the three contact wordings sit behind one link page, which picks among them by who seated the person, and a registration's link opens the new pupil's page or the returning pupil's, two pages picked by whether the address and the name match one confirmed person.\
**See:** the `Einwilligung` · `Kenntnisnahme` entry for the record that stamps a label and what an account-page press stamps.

### `Dublette` — two open applications the triage marks as colliding

**Is:** two or more `eingereicht` applications to one season that collide on a single key, marked side by side for an administrator to choose between. Nothing stores one: the read that serves the triage queue answers which keys collide, and the page marks every row holding one of them.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/schemas.py :: FLBewerbungenListResponse.dubletten_schluessel` is the wire field, composed by `fl_backend/app/api/bewerbungen/services.py :: dubletten_schluessel_of` over `:: build_dubletten_pipeline`. **Trap:** the key is the season and exactly ONE identity — the club id where an applicant picked one, the case-folded Kürzel where it proposes a new school — and never the school's NAME, so two schools of one name asking under different Kürzel are no Dublette, and one club applying in two seasons is two applications rather than one. ### `Subjekt` — the data-protection subject one mailbox stands for

**Is:** which league records a single mailbox matches — its contact seats, its pupil rows and its referee rows, each only once its own person has confirmed it, a pupil's or a referee's only while its row is live, and a seat only while its team has no `austritt` from that season — answered as three lists from three collections at read time, beside `unbestaetigt`, which says that it holds records that could grant a panel and none of them is confirmed yet, `gesperrt`, which says whether the address is on the ban list, and `verwaltung`, the tier of any `Berechtigung` it holds, beside `berechtigt_seit`, when that grant took effect, and `inhaber_seit`, when its `owner` tier did. Nothing stores one, and no row carries the word.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/schemas.py :: FLSubjektResponse`, the endpoint's answer, over `:: FLSubjekt`, the records and the pending flag a person endpoint reads, and `:: FLSubjektSitz`, `:: FLSubjektSpieler` and `:: FLSubjektSchiedsrichter`, composed by `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/crud.py :: find_subjekt` behind `POST /identitaet/subjekt`, whose three reads take the selections a person's own pages take, unnarrowed, and whose judgement `:: _judged` draws every list from the address's own records (`fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/services.py :: eigene_eintraege`), the live ones alone, which `fl_backend/app/shared/einwilligung.py :: is_confirmed` narrows for every caller; the frontend reads it as the generated `fl_frontend/src/core/generated/client/types.ts :: FLSubjektResponse`, and `fl_frontend/src/core/signInGate.ts :: lookUpSubjekt` is the one call reading one, memoised per render, and every frontend reader of a mailbox's records goes through it, the grant read `fl_frontend/src/core/verwaltung.ts :: verwaltungOf` included; the sign-in gate alone reads its own answer instead, flags and no record, through `fl_frontend/src/core/signInGate.ts :: lookUpAnmeldung`.\
**Trap:** it answers RECORDS and never a count of people — each list may be empty and any of them may hold several entries, one person holding seats at two clubs or in several seasons — so a helper counting the answer as people, or reading one entry per list, is wrong. Among pupils one address is one person, enforced by `fl_backend/app/core/constraints.py :: UNIQUE_INDEXES`' `uniq_spieler_email`; across the three collections it is assumed rather than enforced ([`datenschutz.md`](datenschutz.md#5-erasure-reaches-everyone-who-asks)), so a mailbox shared anyway answers two people's records as one. Empty lists are two answers, and only `unbestaetigt` tells them apart: set, the mailbox holds records that could grant a panel and nobody has confirmed any of them; clear, it holds none — a retired row and a seat on a `past` season count as nothing, never as pending. **Empty lists are not an empty account either**: a confirmed retired record, a withdrawn team's seat and a pending application's or registration's are still the address's own records, which `POST /identitaet/anmeldung` answers as `konto` for the sign-in (`fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/crud.py :: find_eigene_eintraege`), so a gate admitting on the lists alone shuts out a person who still holds a consent to take back. Set is not a promise that one press opens a panel: a pupil's stamp is copied from their own confirmed registration only when their team admits it (`fl_backend/app/api/registrierungen/services.py :: compose_person_update`), and a seat an administrator entered is confirmed only through the link its save or a re-send mails, which a seat on a `past` season or of a team that left it is never sent. And it is not yet what a person may do: a seat on a `past` season is still in the list, and `Funktion` is the word for the narrowed answer. `FLSubjektSitz` and `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/schemas.py :: FLLoeschungAnsichtSitz` are two shapes of ONE word: both are per seat rather than per person, and they answer different questions — which seats a mailbox holds across the seasons, and whom an erasure keyed on that address would clear — so one's fields are no guide to the other's. And a grep finds a third site with no data-protection sense at all: `fl_frontend/src/features/aktionen/queries.ts :: readAktionenLogSubjekt` is which ROW's history the action log's query string asks for.\
**See:** the `kontakte` entry above for the seats it lists, and [`datenschutz.md`](datenschutz.md#5-erasure-reaches-everyone-who-asks) for why one address is one subject to the match.

### `Funktion` — what a person is on screen: the record that admits them to a panel

**Is:** one contact seat on a season's team row, one pupil row, one referee row, or administration — each admitting the signed-in person to a panel, the first three derived from their mailbox on every request, administration a stored `Berechtigung` both processes read on every request; plural `Funktionen`, and „Funktion wechseln“ is the switch between two of them.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/crud.py :: funktionen_of` answers what a person endpoint may authorise against, the `Subjekt` answer narrowed by `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/services.py :: grants_a_panel`, which `fl_backend/tests/shared/grants_a_panel.json` holds the frontend's narrowing to; the frontend resolves a person's Funktionen in `fl_frontend/src/core/funktionen.ts :: funktionenOf`, and „Funktion wechseln“ is `fl_frontend/src/shared/components/layout/sidemenu/FunktionSwitcher.tsx :: FunktionSwitcher`; it reads the grant through `fl_frontend/src/core/verwaltung.ts :: verwaltungOf` and judges it with the session in `fl_frontend/src/core/auth.ts :: isAdminSession`; `fl_frontend/src/core/signInGate.ts :: mayReceiveSignIn` reads no Funktion at all, admitting on the `Konto` below.\
**Trap:** `Subjekt` is the wire word and `Funktion` the screen's, and neither crosses: the wire answer still lists a seat on a `past` season, which is no Funktion, so a panel drawn straight from that answer admits a person the backend refuses. It is not `rolle`, which is the captaincy on a squad row and the contact seat on a team row, nor `role`, the session's administrator verdict — a name built from either word reads as one of those. The seasons granting a panel are `active` and `future` alike, a team being entered and acting before its season starts, and a retired or unconfirmed record is no Funktion at all, nor is a seat on a season its team withdrew from, though the same team's seats on its other seasons stand.\
**See:** the `Subjekt` entry above for what the wire answer holds.

### `Konto` — a signed-in person's account, and the records of their own that admit one

**Is:** the account a person signs in to, on screen „Konto“, whose page `/bereich/konto` holds their sign-ins and passkeys, the change of the address they sign in on, and every consent the league stores on a record of their own: one its own person confirmed — a contact seat of any season or of a pending application, a pupil or referee row retired or not, a pending registration its pupil confirmed. Holding such a record admits a sign-in whether or not it grants a panel, and the sign-in gate's answer says so as `konto`.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/services.py :: eigene_eintraege` names an address's own records; `POST /identitaet/anmeldung` answers whether any exists as `fl_backend/app/api/identitaet/schemas.py :: FLAnmeldungResponse`'s `konto`, and `fl_backend/app/api/konto/person_router.py :: get_einwilligungen` serves them; `:: post_adresswechsel` starts the address change. The frontend reads the gate's answer through `fl_frontend/src/core/signInGate.ts :: lookUpAnmeldung` and judges it in `:: signInVerdictOf`; `fl_frontend/src/core/kontoHref.ts :: KONTO_HREF` is the page's address, the one card the landing offers a person holding no Funktion (`fl_frontend/src/features/funktionen/components/views/FunktionenView.tsx`).\
**Trap:** it is not a `Funktion`: a person holding only a `past` season's seat, a retired row or a pending application's seat holds a Konto and no Funktion, and signs in to no panel but the account page, so a gate or a guard asking the Funktion lists whether a person may sign in shuts out one who still holds a consent to take back. And `konto` on the gate's answer is narrower than the screen word: the sign-in library's `user` row is the account itself, per address, and it stands until no league record or grant holds the address, a ban holding none — a record holds it confirmed or not, a pending registration only once its pupil confirmed it, and a replaced address by the notice carrying its account to the new one until that is delivered or given up — when the account sweep deletes it (`fl_frontend/src/features/funktionen/sweep.ts :: runKontoSweep`), while `konto` says only whether a record of the address's own still stands. It names no record, so no sign-in text may tell an address holding one from an address holding none.\
**See:** the `Funktion` entry above for the narrowing that grants a panel, and the `Bereich` entry below for the page's address.

### `inactive_since` — the day something left

**Is:** a nullable `YYYY-MM-DD` string on `teams`, `spieler`, `saison_spieler`, `spielorte` and `schiedsrichter`, where null means current.\
**In code:** the record model of each of those collections (`fl_backend/app/readback.py :: RECORD_MODELS`), which requires the field.\
**Trap:** a date and never a boolean, and on no payload — `DELETE` stamps it, `reactivate` clears it, and the ghost every erased referee's fixtures point at carries it permanently, so no picker offers the ghost — so creating never revives a retired row and a natural-key collision comes back 409; the one other write clearing it is a team's admission of a pupil's registration, which clears the stamp on the person it admits into and rewrites that person's retired squad row of the season rather than writing a second; leaving one season is an `austritt`, a different thing. The German verb pair is fixed: _stilllegen_ retires the entity across the whole league, _austragen_ takes one `saison_spieler` row out of one season's squad — each stamps this field on its own collection, and a name or a sentence about a squad row takes the second verb, never the first (`fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLReplaceSaisonTeamResponse` counts `ausgetragene_squad_rows`). An erasure stamps it on no person's row: it removes the person and their squad rows outright whether or not this field is set, retirement being no step in front of it.

### `Statistik` — the derived league-table figures

**Is:** the league table's figures, computed per team and season from `spiele` on every read.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLTeamStatistik`, built over a read of `spiele` by `fl_backend/app/api/teams/services.py :: build_team_pipeline`, and over fixtures a caller already holds by `fl_backend/app/api/teams/services.py :: build_statistik_by_team` — the bracket resolution's preview.\
**Trap:** nothing stores it, so there is no field to update and nothing to back-fill; a match counts exactly when it carries an `ergebnis`, points come from the season's `rules` rather than a hardcoded 3/1/0, and `statistik_scope` decides which table you get, defaulting to the narrow `gruppenphase` one.

### `mietpreis` · `default_mietpreis` — rental price

**Is:** two fields and never one copy — `mietpreis` is what one fixture paid for its venue, carried on the copy a match embeds; `default_mietpreis` is the venue's own current price — „Mietpreis“ on screen, which the venue list's column shows from `default_mietpreis`. Both are an `int` with `ge=0`.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpielOrtFieldPayload` carries `mietpreis`, and the stored `:: FLSpielOrtField` declares it again over `:: FLSpielOrtFieldPublic`, the base-tier read shape without it; `fl_backend/app/api/spielorte/schemas.py :: FLSpielort` carries `default_mietpreis`.\
**Trap:** `mietpreis` is **submitted** where the `name` and `maps_link` beside it on the same embedded venue are composed by the server — it is what _that_ fixture agreed to pay rather than a copy of anything, which is also why a venue's price change never fans out although its name and `maps_link` do. Neither field carries a Pydantic default, because both patches write their payload back wholesale and a default would overwrite a real rent with `0`.\
**See:** [`domain.md`](domain.md) for the fan-out this is deliberately left out of.

### `payment` · `default_payment` — referee fee, `Honorar` on screen

**Is:** the same split as `mietpreis` — `payment` is what one fixture paid its referee, `default_payment` the referee's own current fee. Both are an `int` with `ge=0`.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/spiele/schemas.py :: FLSpielSchiedsrichterFieldPayload` carries `payment`, and the stored `:: FLSpielSchiedsrichterField` declares it again over the private `:: _SpielSchiedsrichterNamed`, which the base-tier `:: FLSpielSchiedsrichterFieldPublic` extends without a fee or an id; `fl_backend/app/api/schiedsrichter/schemas.py :: FLSchiedsrichter` carries `default_payment`, which reaches the referee's own reads as `honorar` (`:: FLSchiedsrichterSelf`); that German is spelt at each field rather than read from a table, there being one label and not a closed set of them (`fl_frontend/src/features/schiedsrichter/schiedsrichterDraftStatus.ts :: FLSchiedsrichterFieldGroup`).\
**Trap:** no default on either, no fan-out, `payment` stays on the match payload while the `name` beside it is composed, and the base tier is served without it — every one of them for `mietpreis`'s reasons.

### `saison_teams` · `saison_spieler` — the season junctions

**Is:** two collections joined at read time into a team or a player, which is what makes "a team" and "a player" season-scoped at all. Each has a record model of the whole STORED document, which only the read-back reads a row through, while the PROJECTION each `memberships` read serves is a model, and validates the stored rows it carries through it.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/collections.py :: Collection`, with the rows' stored shapes `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLSaisonTeamRecord` and `fl_backend/app/api/spieler/schemas.py :: FLSaisonSpielerRecord`. Each junction's own write endpoints echo the row back — `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLSaisonTeamResponse` and `fl_backend/app/api/spieler/schemas.py :: FLSaisonSpielerResponse`, each assembled from what the write just stored rather than being a model the stored row is read through; the membership projections that DO read stored rows are `fl_backend/app/api/teams/schemas.py :: FLTeamMembership` and `fl_backend/app/api/spieler/schemas.py :: FLSpielerMembership`.\
**Trap:** they differ on the way out — `saison_spieler` carries `inactive_since` because a player leaves a squad — one row at a time by hand, or a whole squad at once when a club is REPLACED in a season, those players not having transferred with the fixtures — and is removed outright only by that person's erasure, while `saison_teams` has no DELETE: a club leaves a season by an `austritt` record, or by a replacement repointing its row at another club, and the row itself survives both. A junction row is addressed under its entity at `/teams/{team_id}/saisons/{saison_id}`, where `saisons` names the junction row rather than a season — except the group swap, which writes two rows at once and so is addressed on the season.

### `Vorgangsnummer` · `trace_id` — the id naming one write on the action log

**Is:** the trace id of the request that made a write, copied off an action-log row to find every row that one save produced (`Aktion` above). nginx mints it per request as thirty-two lowercase hex characters ([the logging overview](logging/overview.md#following-one-request)).\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/core/recording.py :: record_write` stamps it, `fl_backend/app/api/aktionen/schemas.py :: FLAktion` serves it and `fl_backend/app/api/aktionen/admin_router.py :: get_aktionen` takes it as a search term.\
**Trap:** `-nummer` is the trap — the value is a hex string, so nothing about it sorts, increments or bounds a range, an input mask or a validation written for a number refuses the real thing, and one retyped from a screenshot does not survive a `0`/`O` confusion. The German appears in screen copy alone and `trace_id` in code alone, so a search for either spelling finds none of the other's sites. A row no request made carries the sentinel `SYSTEM` in its place (`fl_backend/app/core/logging.py :: NO_REQUEST_SENTINEL`), which gathers every system write rather than one. The same id names every log line of that request across all three surfaces, where the action log answers for recorded writes alone, so an administrator searching it and whoever reads the container logs are asking two different questions. And the rows it names are expired twelve months after the write they recorded, so one quoted in a support request has a shelf life.\
**See:** [the logging overview](logging/overview.md#following-one-request) for who mints the id and what becomes of a client's own, and the `Aktion` entry for what one trace gathers.

### `Zustellstand` — what became of the last message to one recipient

**Is:** what the mail provider reported about one message the Postausgang sent — one of the five states a delivery event reports (`zugestellt`, `verzoegert`, `unzustellbar`, `unterdrueckt` and `beschwerde`), the provider's token for a refusal, and the instant it reported — held on that mail's own `postausgang` row and null until the first event arrives. Beside it the row's `stand` says what the outbox did with the mail: `gesendet` where the provider took it, and `zurueckgehalten`, `gesperrt`, `entfallen` or `aufgegeben` where it never left. An editor reads a record's seat by its newest row, the two together.\
**In code:** `fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/schemas.py :: FLPostausgangZustellung` over `:: FLPostausgangZustellStand`, at the `zustellung` of the `postausgang` row the message was sent from, beside the row's own `stand`; written by `fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/system_router.py :: post_ereignis`, and read beside a link as `fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/schemas.py :: FLPostausgangLage`. The provider's report reaches it through `fl_frontend/src/app/api/mail/zustellung/route.ts`, which finds the row by the id the send carries as its `fl_frontend/src/core/mailKinds.ts :: POSTAUSGANG_TAG` tag, and acknowledges a report without that tag, about no row, unrecorded.\
**Trap:** it is not the seat's `Stand`, which is what the PERSON did — a seat reads `bestaetigt` with an earlier link that bounced, and folding the two would make that state unrepresentable. Three of the five, with a row the outbox gave up, are what a reader takes as the mail not having arrived — `unzustellbar`, `unterdrueckt` and `beschwerde` (`fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/schemas.py :: FLPostausgangLage.unzustellbar`) — while `verzoegert` is a delayed message still arriving. The provider sends events in any order, so an event moves the state only from the states `fl_backend/app/api/postausgang/services.py :: FOLGT_AUF` lists before it and never back from a later one. `grund` is the provider's own token and never its prose, whose bounce message quotes the recipient's address. A re-send owes a row of its own, so a refusal stays with the mail it was about and never outlives the address that mail went to: the newest row speaks for the seat. On screen a delivery draws nothing, being the ordinary course, and only a state an administrator acts on draws a chip (`fl_frontend/src/features/bewerbungen/zustellLage.ts :: zustellungChip`): a mail the outbox gave up reads „Nicht gesendet“.\
**See:** the `Bestätigung` entry for the links whose mails it reports on.

### `Bereich` — the address segment every signed-in person's pages sit under

**Is:** `/bereich`, the first path segment of every page a person reaches by signing in. Four words follow it for the four kinds of person a panel serves: `admin` for the administrator, `team` for a team's contact seats, `spieler` for a player and `schiedsrichter` for a referee. `team` carries its season, `team/<team_id>/<saison_id>`, because one team holds seats in two seasons. A fifth word, `konto`, is the signed-in person's one account page: it is the person's rather than any Funktion's, so it stands once, under the person area, whichever area links it, and a person holding a Konto and no Funktion signs in to it alone.\
**In code:** `fl_frontend/src/app/bereich/`, whose `admin` tree is the administrator's panel. `fl_frontend/src/proxy.ts :: config` matches `/bereich`, which Next tests raw and decoded, all but a prefetch carrying a cookie named as a session's, a foreign one included; the proxy turns away a request carrying no session cookie, under every word, `admin` included, and judges nothing past it. `fl_frontend/src/app/robots.ts` disallows `/bereich` whole, the landing's own address included, so a new word takes no crawl rule of its own.\
**Trap:** the proxy turning a request away is not the proxy guarding the page — it judges nothing past whether a session cookie is carried, not whether its session stands, the factor, the grant or the ban, so a page is served to any request carrying one unless the page reads its own session: no layout turns a session away. A cookie no check serves, an ended session's or a code-borne administrator's, meets the area's frame and the page's static chrome before the page's own check sends it on. The words are addresses and not screen copy: `admin` is English beside three German ones, and `team` names a club's seats in one season rather than the season-independent `Team` document. The panel's old address `/admin` answers 404 with no redirect, by the rule a renamed page follows. On screen the word is the place's name („Dein Bereich“, „Zu Deinem Bereich“), so a sentence using it for one part of a page reads as the whole area: the shared crash panel of `fl_frontend/src/features/dashboard/components/ui/DashboardError.tsx` says „Diese Seite“ for that reason.\
**See:** [`frontend/overview.md`](frontend/overview.md#authentication-and-authorization) for the layering.

### `Tabelle` — the league table, and the page showing it

**Is:** the screen word for one season's standings, and for the public page showing them, whose address segment is `tabelle`.\
**In code:** `fl_frontend/src/core/routes.ts :: ROUTES.tabelle`, which gives the page its address and its name; the figures are the `Statistik` entry's.

### `Kontakt` — the page saying how to reach the league

**Is:** the screen word for the public page listing the league's contact channels, at `/kontakt`.\
**In code:** `fl_frontend/src/app/(public)/(meta)/kontakt/page.tsx` over `fl_frontend/src/features/meta/components/views/KontaktView.tsx`; `fl_frontend/src/core/routes.ts :: ROUTES.kontakt` gives its address.

### `K.-o.-Runde` · `K.-o.-Baum` — the knockout stage, and its tree

**Is:** „K.-o.-Runde“ is the screen word for a season's knockout stage, every round after the `gruppenphase`, and for its public page, whose address segment is `ko-runde`; „K.-o.-Baum“ is the tree its fixtures are drawn as.\
**In code:** `fl_frontend/src/core/copy.ts :: COPY.shared.kORunde` and `:: COPY.shared.kOBaum`; `fl_frontend/src/core/routes.ts :: ROUTES.kORunde` gives the page its address.
